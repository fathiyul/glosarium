Affiliate ROI = revenue attributed to partner links/codes ÷ partner cost (commission, fees, sometimes sample products and flat creator payments). Superficially the cleanest performance channel — you mostly pay on completed sales. The reality has a structural bias baked into last-click attribution.

```text
customer's actual path: saw creator → thought about it →
  googled "brandname voucher" → clicked coupon-site link →
  bought

coupon site took the last click → gets the commission →
  their "ROI" looks world-beating → budget follows
```

Coupon and deal-site partners harvest *committed* demand — the purchase was coming — and the model pays them as if they created it. Same dynamic for brand-keyword affiliates bidding on your name. Every affiliate ROI comparison that lets the affiliate choose attribution rules is self-reporting: the channel whose economics depend on claiming the last click is reporting its own value.

The disciplined reads (analysts and growth-stage founders):

- **Holdouts per partner class**, not per partner: pause the coupon sites in a region for a period and watch total revenue. If it barely moves, that partner's ROI is harvesting ([holdback group](/t/holdback-group)).
- **Incremental ROI over reported ROI** ([incremental revenue](/t/incremental-revenue)): for KOL/creator posts, reported numbers are inflated by the same last-click problem plus promo-code leakage (codes surfacing on deal pages the creator never mentioned).
- **Tier the commission to the funnel role**: creators doing genuine discovery (new-customer rate, first orders from that link) deserve rates that harvesting cannot justify. Track first-purchase share per partner as the quality signal.

KOL/creator specifics: engagement [metrics without code data are story](/t/vanity-metrics)-adjacent; use unique codes and windows; watch for fake-audience inflation on smaller creators (order spikes with return/abandonment anomalies). And the trade-off: affiliates bought you reach you couldn't afford — the question at scale is never "does the channel earn" but "at what marginal return," because top creators' harvestable demand caps fast.
