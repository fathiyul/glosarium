ROAS = revenue attributed to an ad ÷ that ad's spend. Rp 8,000,000 from Rp 2,000,000 of spend → ROAS 4×. Clean formula, and the two words that quietly ruin decisions: **"attributed"**.

Founders at PMF should first ask the margin question: ROAS is a revenue ratio, and ads are a cost. At 50% gross margin, ROAS 4× means Rp 2 of margin per Rp 1 of spend — profitable; at 20% margin the same 4× is Rp 0.80 per rupiah spent — losing money per order at a respectable-looking ratio. Break-even ROAS is just the inverse of margin (1 ÷ gross margin %); anything below it buys unprofitable growth. Compare with the channel's [CPA and unit economics](/t/unit-economics), not against other teams' screenshots.

Analysts should second-guess the attribution: a 4× reported ROAS is *platform-claimed* revenue from *platform-chosen* touchpoints. Each platform counts its own contribution generously ([attribution](/t/attribution)); summing their ROAS reports can claim more of your revenue than exists. Discount for what would have sold anyway — only [incremental revenue](/t/incremental-revenue) is real return, which a holdout test measures; reported ROAS is the optimistic version of it.

The failure pattern that repeats: scale the campaign with the best reported ROAS, which is usually bottom-funnel or branded-search harvesting people already coming. True incremental ROAS flattens immediately while spend doubles.

When ROAS *is* the right boss: steady-state performance channels with real margin math behind them, compared within the same attribution rules. When not: brand launches and new-market entry, where early ROAS measures the *most harvestable* sliver of demand, not the size of the prize.
