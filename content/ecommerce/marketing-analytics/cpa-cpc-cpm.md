Three prices for the same ad, differing in **what you pay on**:

```text
CPM  cost per 1,000 impressions   → you pay for being seen
CPC  cost per click               → you pay for interest
CPA  cost per action (order/signup)→ you pay for results
```

They connect through the funnel. If a placement charges Rp 40,000 CPM and your [CTR](/t/ctr) is 1%, each click costs Rp 40,000 ÷ (1,000 × 0.01) = Rp 4,000. If your landing page converts 2.5% of clicks, the **CPA** is Rp 4,000 ÷ 0.025 = Rp 160,000 per order. The chain makes the tradeoffs legible: a pricier placement (higher CPM) can be cheaper per order when its audience clicks and buys more.

Analysts: CPA is the one to defend in meetings because it is the only one with revenue attached — but note how differently the platforms define it (checkout order? purchase confirmation? attributed within which window?). Two dashboards with two "CPAs" for one campaign is normal; make the definitions match before comparing channels.

Founders: bid models exist because risk transfers. CPM (pay for reach, most risk on you) → CPC (platform shares interest risk) → CPA/oCPA (platform takes results risk, priced accordingly, and quietly optimizes toward whoever guarantees volume — the junk-COD-order problem in some channels is exactly this incentive biting). Never conclude "CPA is cheaper therefore buy it"; check order *quality* per model, especially refunds and rejections.

When the low-CPM temptation misleads: cheap impressions nobody acts on are not cheap reach, they're cheap noise; when CPM is right: brand and launch moments where being seen *is* the goal — but then measure it separately from [ROAS](/t/roas), which will look ugly by design.
