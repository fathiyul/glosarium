CSAT asks one thing right after an interaction — *"how satisfied were you with [this delivery / this chat / this return]?"* — usually a 1–5 scale, reported as the share of top-box responses (4–5) or the mean. It is the *transaction-level* experience score: narrow, immediate, and only as meaningful as the moment it interrupts.

```text
CSAT by touchpoint, monthly:
  post-delivery survey    4.38 / top-box 71%
  post-chat               4.10        66%
  post-return             3.20        41%  ← where to look first
```

The moment-choice is everything: CSAT after a resolved ticket measures support; after delivery, it measures logistics; after a *failed* delivery it measures anger at a specific event — and averaging the three into one "store CSAT" is how teams get a number nobody trusts and nobody can act on. Report by *event*, track trend per event, and watch the response population: survey respondents skew to the delighted and the furious; the silent middle is where [churn](/t/churn-rate) actually grows.

Why it beats its reputation as a vanity metric: CSAT is a *leading indicator of renewals and repeat rate* at the interaction level — customers whose delivery CSAT dips after a strong history churn measurably more, and CSAT attached to the *specific* courier lane or ticket category is one JOIN away from the operational culprit. That join is the whole craft: a satisfaction score that never meets an order, a lane, or a ticket ID is a mood, not a measurement.

The NPS sibling ([net promoter score](/t/net-promoter-score)) asks about the *relationship*; effort ([CES](/t/ces)) asks about the *friction*. A team with all three learns they disagree — "satisfied but effortful" is a customer who rates you 5 and shops around next month — and those disagreements are the honest experience picture.

When CSAT misleads: post-*recovery* surveys (customers whose problem you fixed rate ecstatically — the "service recovery paradox" flatters the team that caused the problem), low response windows, and markets where one-tap ratings are habitually generous; segment before comparing regions.
