Assortment gap = demand the market demonstrates that your catalog cannot serve. It is found in three places, each with a different reliability:

```text
1. zero-result / low-result site search  — YOUR customers
   asking for what you lack (loudest signal, smallest sample)
2. competitor/marketplace listings       — what sells elsewhere
   that you don't carry (broad, needs demand proof)
3. category penetration vs benchmark     — categories your
   customers buy a lot and you under-carry
```

The method's core discipline is **demand proof before SKU count**: the gap list is a candidate *hypothesis* deck, not a purchase order. Score candidates on: search volume behind them, competitor velocity proxies (reviews-per-time, best-seller ranks — noisy but directional), margin shape ([margin per SKU](/t/margin-per-sku) of the target category), and *fit with your catalog's gravity* — a store known for kitchen that adds phone cases buys listings, not customers ([category penetration](/t/category-penetration) of the adjacent buyer tells you which additions compound).

The internal mirror most teams skip: your own **zero-result searches** are free, real, and from people already in the store — a weekly ranked list of unfulfilled queries is the single highest-yield assortment brief in e-commerce, and the same list catches catalog *merchandise-ability* gaps (in stock but unfindable: wrong title attributes, missing variants) which need taxonomy fixes, not purchasing.

Founders: assortment gaps are how categories *choose you* — a "completeness" reputation compounds (search share, [gateway](/t/gateway-category) breadth, one-stop retention) — but every addition carries [tail cost](/t/long-tail-contribution): capital, listings, returns risk. The grid answer is a stated policy: "we add categories only where ≥X% of our buyers already buy it elsewhere, or where search demand exceeds Y" — and re-run quarterly because both numbers drift.

Cautions: competitor price/stock scraping has terms-of-service and legal limits ([web scraping](/t/web-scraping)); and gaps defined against the *wrong* competitor (a different city-tier, a different price band) produce confidently wrong briefs.
