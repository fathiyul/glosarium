Margin per SKU = selling price − COGS − every variable cost attributable to the unit: payment fees, shipping subsidy share, packaging, marketplace commission, expected return cost, and the voucher actually funded. Where revenue ranks products by volume of exchange, margin ranks them by what survives the exchange — and the two lists disagree loudest exactly where a store's growth story is weakest.

```text
SKU         revenue   realized margin
voucher-fed bestseller   100%        6%
"slow" accessory          18%       44%
the hero pays rent; the slow one pays you
```

The build discipline is allocation, and it must be *stated* before results are read:

- **Shipping**: per-parcel cost attributed to orders, then split by weight/value rule — flat-per-SKU hides that heavy-cheap items are the loss leaders nobody voted for.
- **Returns**: expected loss = return rate × (reverse cost + restocking loss), per SKU ([return rate](/t/return-rate)'s real job).
- **Discounts**: at *order* level, not SKU level — a Rp 50k off on a basket lands on everything inside it.

The classic failures: pricing decisions made on **gross margin** (commission- and COD-cost-blind — the two that quietly eat Indonesian marketplace margins), "profitable" SKUs that ignore their *support* ticket load (a per-order cost the margin sheet never saw), and hero-SKU rankings rebuilt after a finance pass that reallocate shipping by revenue share instead of mass.

Founders: the margin-per-SKU view powers the two highest-leverage merchandising moves — the [attach-rate](/t/attach-rate) push (pair high-margin accessories into hero baskets) and the delist/renegotiate list (deep-negative-margin SKUs buying attention that never repeats). It is also the honest input to [price elasticity](/t/price-elasticity) tests: raise the inelastic-and-thin first, cut where margin can survive the tide.

When the math is unknowable: shared promo budgets and brand halo (ads lifted *all* SKUs) — allocate with a rule and a footnote; false precision here argues louder than the data.
