Market share = your category revenue (or GMV/orders) ÷ total market category volume. The numerator you own; the denominator is the entire discipline — platform category reports, [scraped competitor velocities](/t/web-scraping), analyst panels, or your own TAM/SAM model, and each produces a *different* share from the same store. "12% market share" without a named denominator is a mood, not a metric (see [TAM/SAM/SOM](/t/tam-sam-som) for the construction).

```text
same store, three denominators:
  "phone accessories, national GMV (platform report)"   → 2.1%
  "the 3 marketplaces we sell on, category GMV"          → 8.4%
  "comparable-price-band sellers in Jabodetabek"         → 19%
all true; only one answers "where can we win?"
```

The definition choices that make share *decision-grade*: **market** (category AND price band AND region — a premium Jakarta seller's national share is diluted by a million Rp 20k orders it never competes for), **unit** (GMV share for platform-game strategy, revenue share for P&L, *margin* share for the questions nobody asks: you can gain share and lose the profit race), **period** (share is seasonal in Ramadan categories; rolling-12-month or bust).

Why track it at all when the data is fuzzy: share's directional trend with a *consistent* (even imperfect) denominator is the few metrics that separates "our store grew" from "the market grew." [Revenue growth](/t/revenue-decomposition) of 30% against a market at 45% is losing. That sentence justifies an imprecise number; it also means: pick ONE denominator source and never switch mid-trend without annotating every historical point.

Founders: share framing decides *which market you're in* — a seller at 2% of national fashion is invisible; at 22% of "hijab sport-category, tier-2 cities" they're the leader. [Counter-positioning](/t/counter-positioning) moves live inside those reframes — the number is a strategy instrument before it is a scoreboard.
