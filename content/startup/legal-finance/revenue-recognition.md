---
title: "Revenue Recognition"
summary: "Rules for when revenue counts as earned, such as a yearly prepayment recognized month by month."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Revenue recognition is the rulebook for when earned money becomes *revenue on the books* — under the international framework (IFRS 15, and its US twin ASC 606), you recognize revenue as the performance obligation is satisfied, not when the invoice lands or the cash arrives. A yearly SaaS prepayment billed January 1 is twelve months of revenue, roughly a twelfth each month; the January cash is not January revenue.

The three-number reality most decks blur: [cash](/t/cash-flow) from billings, bookings as sales performance, and recognized revenue as the earned number ([bookings, billings and revenue](/t/bookings-billings-revenue) keeps them straight). They diverge most in prepay-heavy, multi-element or enterprise deals — exactly the deals where founders want the biggest number to win.

The mechanics that trip companies up:

- **Bundles and allocations.** Product + implementation + support sold together must split price across obligations; "all upfront" is rarely the right answer and auditors check.
- **Variable consideration.** Discounts, credits and usage tiers must be estimated and trueed up.
- **Agent vs principal.** Marketplaces and resellers recognize take-rate revenue, not gross flow-through ([take-rate](/t/take-rate) is the difference between "we processed $10M" and "we earned $800K") — a classic [vanity metric](/t/vanity-metrics) trap with a legal edge.

When it bites: [financial audits](/t/financial-audit), investor diligence, and any acquisition — misstated revenue is the finding that erodes trust fastest because it is not a judgment call, it's a rule. When you can keep it simple: monthly subscriptions with same-period service are genuinely one-twelfths, and steady-state monthly businesses converge cash, billings and revenue anyway.

Set the policy early, apply it consistently, and label every revenue number in front of investors with which of the three it is.
