A **trademark** protects the names, logos and slogans identifying your product from confusingly similar use by others — and, symmetrically, the search before adopting a name protects you from infringing someone else's mark. It is the cheapest brand insurance available, and the most skipped until a cease-and-desist (or a rebrand mid-traction) teaches its value.

```text
before naming:  search registries + web + app stores for conflicts (in markets you'll enter)
after naming:   file in home market early; extend to expansion markets before entering
```

Search first in every market that matters: a clear name locally can collide in the US, EU or neighboring SEA countries, and rebranding after launch costs far more than checking upfront. File at home early (costs are modest); extend as expansion firms up.

The classic mistakes:

- Falling in love with an unusable name. Months of brand attachment to something unregistrable or conflicting. Clear the name before the logo, the domain celebration and the merch.
- Confusing company, product and domain names. The PT name, the brand and the .com can differ — and each needs its own clearance. Check all three, protect what customers see.
- Descriptive names that cannot protect. "FastPay" describes; distinctive marks protect. Coined or suggestive names (arbitrary to the product) register strongest — factor this into naming, not after.
- Ignoring lookalikes in adjacent classes. Trademark scope runs by goods/services classes; a clash in yours blocks you even from a different industry's famous mark direction. Search your classes specifically.
- No monitoring after filing. Registration without watching lets infringers entrench. Watch key markets periodically; enforce early and proportionally (a letter before a lawsuit).

**Budget it with formation costs**, not as a later luxury. A cleared, filed mark in home plus expansion markets is a quiet asset acquirers and partners both check.
