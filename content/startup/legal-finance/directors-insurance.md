---
title: "D&O Insurance"
summary: "Liability insurance for directors and officers, often required before a priced round."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

D&O (directors and officers) insurance covers the personal liability of your board and executives if they are sued over decisions made for the company — investor claims, allegations of misleading disclosures, employment-practice complaints, regulatory actions. In practice it arrives with the first priced round: investors with seats are not joining a board whose members carry uninsurable personal risk, so the policy is a condition, not a perk ([term sheet](/t/term-sheet) logistics, [board of directors](/t/board-of-directors) hygiene).

The coverage shape: it protects *the individuals* when the company can't or shouldn't indemnify them fully, and it also reimburses the company where indemnification is permitted. What it does not do is cover fraud or personal profit by the insured — insurance for the honest mistakes and the litigious exposure, not the criminal ones.

The founder-level traps:

- Buying late or skipping to save a few thousand dollars, then learning the hard way that institutional directors will not join uninsured — and that claims *after* a filing event cannot be covered by a policy bought afterward.
- Reading only the premium. Coverage limits, retentions (the deductible before insurance pays), exclusions, and — importantly — run-off (continuing cover for past decisions after someone leaves the board) decide whether the policy is real.
- Ignoring side-A versus side-C nuances: when the company itself is insolvent and indemnification fails, the personal cover is the whole point. That is also the moment the board most needs it, which is why claims-made policies and careful wording matter more during good quarters than bad ones.

When not to obsess: pre-seed, all-founder boards with no outside money usually have little insurable event risk — though a claims-made policy started *before* trouble exists is cheap insurance against future regret. The moment outside investors, a real [data room](/t/data-room) and formal board meetings appear, treat D&O as table stakes, and ask your lawyer — jurisdictional specifics vary.
