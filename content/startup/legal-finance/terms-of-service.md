**Terms of Service (ToS)** are the contract users accept by using your product: what you provide, what they may and may not do, how and when you can suspend them, limits of your liability, and how disputes resolve. Combined with the [privacy policy](/t/privacy-policy), they form the legal interface of the product — presented at signup, enforced in disputes.

```text
cover:  service description + acceptable use + accounts and payment + IP/licence back
        + warranties disclaimed (as allowed) + liability caps + termination + governing law
```

Draft from reality like the privacy policy: describe actual behavior (uptime efforts, support scope, data handling) rather than aspirational promises that become breach claims. Consumer and B2B terms differ substantially — the latter negotiated, the former take-it-or-leave-it.

The classic mistakes:

- No terms at all. Operating on goodwill means every dispute starts from zero, and payment processors, app stores and enterprise buyers all ask eventually. Publish before you need them.
- Copied terms contradicting behavior. A template promising arbitration in another country, or uptime you do not provide. Tailor to reality with counsel.
- Unilateral-change overreach. Reserving the right to change anything anytime breeds distrust and, in some jurisdictions, unenforceability. Change transparently with notice for material terms.
- Hiding the sharp edges. Auto-renewals, data deletion on cancellation, price-change rights — buried clauses that ambush users generate chargebacks and regulator attention. Surface them plainly.
- One global ToS for every market. Consumer-protection, language and enforceability rules differ (Indonesia, EU and others each have mandatory notes). Localize where you operate materially.

**Pair with:** the privacy policy (data half) and, for business customers, a negotiated [MSA](/t/master-service-agreement). Review terms yearly or on every business-model change — whichever comes first.
