A **master service agreement (MSA)** is the umbrella contract for a business relationship: core terms (services, fees, IP, confidentiality, liability, termination) agreed once, with individual orders/statements of work layered for each purchase. It turns every future deal into paperwork-light execution instead of renegotiation.

```text
MSA (once):  liability caps · IP · confidentiality · termination · governing law
orders (each): scope · price · timeline — referencing the MSA, signed fast
```

Have your own MSA ready before enterprise sales starts: proposing your paper frames terms favorably and accelerates procurement dramatically versus redlining every customer's forty-page form from scratch. Legal Oft-cited rule holds — he who drafts, wins (within reason and reputation).

The classic mistakes:

- No paper, handshake enterprise. Work starting on emails and calls, terms disputed mid-delivery when leverage has shifted. Nothing material starts without signed MSA + order.
- Unlimited liability accepted casually. Caps at fees-paid (or a multiple) are standard; uncapped liability for a startup is existential risk per contract. Cap everything, carve out only what law or leverage truly requires.
- Auto-renewal traps (both directions). Customer contracts auto-renewing into unwanted commitments, or your revenue churning on missed renewals. Calendar every term; renegotiate deliberately.
- SLA promises without operational backing. Uptime and response commitments the team cannot meet become breach claims. Promise what operations deliver ([SLA](/t/sla)), instrument proof, staff to it.
- IP ownership ambiguity. Work product, customizations, data — who owns what after termination must be explicit. Default to customer owning their data always, work product per negotiation, with extraction assistance defined.

**Pair with:** [DPA](/t/data-processing-agreement) for data, order forms for scope, and counsel review before signing anything structural. The MSA is infrastructure — build it once, reuse it for years.
