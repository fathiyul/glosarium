**Cash accounting** records when money moves; **accrual accounting** records when value is earned or owed, regardless of movement. A December invoice paid in February is December revenue under accrual, February revenue under cash. Early startups often run cash-basis (simple, matches the bank); growth, investors and auditors demand accrual (matches reality).

```text
cash:     invoice Dec, paid Feb → February revenue (bank says so)
accrual:  invoice Dec, paid Feb → December revenue + receivable (earned says so)
```

Accrual answers "how is the business doing?" (matching revenue to effort and period); cash answers "can we pay Friday?" Both questions matter, so mature companies keep accrual books *and* watch cash flow — the basis answers different questions, and confusing them misleads in both directions.

The classic mistakes:

- Cash books hiding rot. Booming cash from annual prepayments masking collapsing renewals underneath. Accrual (especially deferred revenue tracking) reveals what cash conceals.
- Accrual complexity premature. Full accrual machinery for a pre-revenue team burns accountant hours on precision nobody uses. Match method to stage: cash early, accrual as revenue, contracts and diligence demand it.
- Tax-method mismatch. Some jurisdictions constrain which basis small businesses may use, and switching has consequences. Decide with the accountant, not by vibe.
- Receivables/payables blindness under cash. Cash books ignore what is owed both directions until it moves — then everything arrives at once. Even cash-basis teams track aging schedules separately.
- Revenue games under accrual. Channel stuffing, bill-and-hold, creative cutoffs — accrual's judgment calls are gameable exactly where incentives point. Pair with [revenue recognition](/t/revenue-recognition) discipline and audits as you scale.

**Default path:** cash simplicity while tiny, accrual books once revenue, contracts or investors require them — with cash-flow watching throughout regardless of basis. See [P&L](/t/profit-and-loss) for reading accrual output.
