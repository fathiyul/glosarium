A young company typically meets four tax fronts: corporate income tax on profits, VAT/sales tax on transactions (collected via [PKP/PPN](/t/pkp-ppn)-style status), payroll taxes on salaries, and withholding taxes on payments to vendors, contractors and foreigners ([PPh](/t/pph-withholding) in Indonesia). None cares that you are busy disrupting things; all compound when ignored.

```text
standing duties:  withhold correctly every payment → remit on schedule → file periodically
                  → keep records that survive audit → review yearly with an accountant
```

The regime details — rates, thresholds, deadlines — move by country and year; this page deliberately quotes none. What does not move: the obligation exists from the first payment, penalties dwarf the original amounts, and clean records make every interaction (filings, fundraising diligence, audits) boring instead of terrifying.

The classic mistakes:

- "Too small to matter." Tax obligations trigger on activity, not comfort. Backdated bills land with penalties precisely when cash is tightest.
- DIY across complexity. One country, simple revenue — manageable with software plus annual advice. Cross-border, multi-entity, or regulated: professional from the start, priced against catastrophe.
- Withholding blindness. The most common startup tax failure worldwide: paying gross and discovering the withholding duty years later. Determine every payment type's treatment once, systematize forever.
- Choosing entity for tax alone. Tax-optimized structures that complicate fundraising, hiring or operations cost more than they save. Optimize taxes within a fundable, operable structure — not instead of one.
- No calendar. Filing dates live nowhere, discovered by penalty letter. Maintain a tax calendar with alerts; assign one owner.

**Get an accountant before you need one** — at formation ideally, at first revenue at the latest. Monthly books, correct withholding, filed returns: the unglamorous trio that keeps the company alive to disrupt another day. Data protection law rewards the same discipline (see [UU PDP](/t/uu-pdp)).
