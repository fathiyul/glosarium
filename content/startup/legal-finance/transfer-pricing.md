---
title: "Transfer Pricing"
summary: "Pricing transactions between your own companies in different countries, which tax authorities scrutinize."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: scale }
---

Transfer pricing is the tax rules for trade inside your own company: when your Singapore parent bills your Indonesian subsidiary for services, or the entity holding your IP licenses it to operating companies, the price charged must match what unrelated parties would agree — an *arm's length* price. Tax authorities care because the price is a dial for moving profit between low-tax and high-tax countries, and they treat it as a dial someone else is turning on their revenue.

For a founder with a [holding structure](/t/singapore-holding-structure), this becomes real when: the holding company charges management or service fees to operating entities; a local entity pays royalties or license fees to a related IP holder; cross-border intra-group invoices carry withholding ([Indonesia PPh withholding](/t/pph-withholding) applies to many such payments — treaty relief can reduce it if paperwork is right); or you hire in-country and the group charges "support" without substance behind it.

The compliance spine:

- **Documentation.** Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation — what services, how priced, benchmark support — is required in many jurisdictions above related-party thresholds, and the thresholds and formats change: check current local rules with a tax advisor.
- **Substance.** An entity that employs nobody and decides nothing cannot justify charging a lot; paper structures without substance are the first thing examiners dismantle.
- **Consistency.** Prices that swing year to year invite questions regardless of the paperwork.

The classic mistakes: pricing intra-group services at "whatever cash is spare," which Indonesian, Malaysian or Vietnamese tax authorities read as profit shifting, with penalties that exceed the back-tax; and discovering the structure only when a big [exit](/t/exit-strategy) or [audit](/t/financial-audit) surfaces the intercompany mess in [due diligence](/t/investor-due-diligence).

When it does not bind you: one entity, one country, no intercompany trade — and keep it that way until the growth actually needs the structure. When the structure does appear, transfer pricing is the cost of keeping it, not paperwork on top.
