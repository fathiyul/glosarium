title: "Financial Audit"
summary: "An independent check of your financial statements, often required by investors or law."

---

A financial audit is an independent examination of your financial statements by a licensed auditor, ending in an opinion on whether they present the numbers fairly under the applicable accounting rules. It is not a review, not a bookkeeping cleanup, and not something your accountant's "compiled" statements substitute for — each level of assurance is different, and sophisticated readers ask which one they are getting ([bookkeeping](/t/bookkeeping) is the base layer, not the ceiling).

Founders meet audits when someone with power requires it: institutional investors negotiating audit rights, lenders, government tenders, an [IPO](/t/ipo) or acquisition [due diligence](/t/investor-due-diligence), or the law itself — several jurisdictions require statutory audits above size thresholds or for licensed businesses (fintechs under [OJK supervision](/t/ojk-licensing), for example), and the thresholds and rules move; confirm yours with an accountant, not a blog.

What founders get wrong:

- Waiting until the demand letter arrives. An audit of messy [cash-vs-accrual accounting](/t/cash-vs-accrual-accounting) becomes a reconstruction project: you will pay audit fees and accounting fees to build the records the audit examines.
- Treating [revenue recognition](/t/revenue-recognition) as a presentation choice. Subscription models with prepayments, multi-element deals and discounts are exactly where audits surface the gap between pitch numbers and accounting numbers — the earlier the honest mapping, the less painful the finding.
- Assuming one audit implies the next. Auditors need clean opening balances; skipping a year creates a scar you pay twice to remove.

An audit buys you credibility capital — a [data room](/t/data-room) that holds, a board that trusts the [runway](/t/runway) math, acquirers who cannot argue the numbers. When your stage genuinely needs none of that, defer; when a term sheet mentions audit rights, budget it before the round closes, not after.
