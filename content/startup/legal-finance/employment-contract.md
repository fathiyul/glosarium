An **employment contract** sets compensation, role, confidentiality, IP ownership, non-compete boundaries (where enforceable), termination terms and working arrangement. For startups it does double duty: protecting the company (IP, confidentiality, non-solicitation) and selling the candidate (clarity signals seriousness to people leaving safe jobs).

```text
must cover:  role + compensation (gross AND net) · IP assignment · confidentiality
             termination + notice · equity terms if any · governing law
```

Use local templates reviewed by local counsel — employment law varies enormously (Indonesia's [PKWT/PKWTT](/t/pkwt-pkwtt) fixed-term vs permanent distinction, probation rules, severance). A US template applied in Jakarta (or vice versa) misstates rights both sides rely on.

The classic mistakes:

- Verbal offers with details "to follow". Salary misunderstandings (gross vs net!), role ambiguity and start-date drift all breed before day one. Written offer before resignation from their old job — always.
- Missing IP/confidentiality clauses. The default hire without assignment leaves inventions ambiguous. Every contract assigns IP and binds confidentiality ([IP assignment](/t/ip-assignment)).
- Overreaching restrictions. Absurd non-competes and perpetual non-solicits repel good hires and often fail legally. Restrict narrowly what genuinely needs protecting.
- Probation without process. Probation periods need evaluation criteria and check-ins to be useful (and in some jurisdictions, to be lawful). Define success for the first 90 days in the contract's shadow, reviewed monthly.
- Equity promises in prose. "You'll get options later" without plan, strike mechanics or timeline is a future dispute. Paper equity with the grant or do not mention it.

**Template once, review per hire:** standard contract reviewed by counsel, individualized annexes per role. See [contractors](/t/contractor-agreement) for the non-employee version and [PKWT/PKWTT](/t/pkwt-pkwtt) for Indonesia's contract types.
