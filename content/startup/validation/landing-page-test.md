A **landing page test** describes the product on one page — headline, problem, solution sketch, signup form — then buys or borrows traffic and measures conversion. A weekend of work answers "does anyone care?" before months of building answer it expensively.

```text
page (proposition + email form) → e.g. 500 visits from ads/communities → 40 signups (8%)
read:  message-market match signal, NOT product validation
```

What it validates is the message and the channel, not the product. High conversion means the promise resonates and the traffic source works; it says nothing about retention, willingness to pay, or whether you can build it. Treat it as the first filter, never the last.

The classic mistakes:

- Traffic that flatters. Sending it to friends, followers and Product Hunt measures your network, not the market. Test on cold traffic resembling real buyers.
- Asking for nothing. A page with no email field and "contact us" converts browsers into ghosts. Demand contact details — the friction is the measurement.
- Declaring victory at any number. 8% of 500 means something; 50% of 12 means nothing. Size the traffic so the result could actually disappoint you.
- One variant, one audience. A failing page might be a failing message, a wrong audience, or a weak offer — indistinguishable in one test. Vary headline, audience and ask across runs.
- Never following up. Signups rotting in a spreadsheet teach nothing. Email them, interview them, [pre-sell](/t/pre-selling) them — the list is the beginning of customer development, not its end.

**Budget it honestly:** a few hundred visits from representative traffic, a clear ask, and a follow-up plan. Then graduate successful pages to concierge tests and pre-sales.
