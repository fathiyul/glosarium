**Early adopters** are customers whose pain is so acute they will use an unfinished, buggy, ugly product — and help shape it. They do not need convincing; they need relief. Every startup's first ten customers should come from this group, because only they tolerate version 0.1 long enough to teach you what 1.0 must be.

```text
mainstream:  needs polish, proof, references ("call me when it works")
early adopter: has the problem NOW, tried workarounds, will co-build ("when can I start?")
```

Find them where pain concentrates: complaints in communities, elaborate spreadsheet workarounds, recently funded teams with new mandates, users of a dying competitor. They self-identify by effort already spent — look for the workarounds, not the demographics.

The classic mistakes:

- Treating all users as equal evidence. Mainstream polite interest means nothing; early-adopter obsessive use means everything. Weight feedback by the speaker's pain, not volume.
- Building for the mainstream too early. Polish for people who will never buy v0.1 wastes the window when early adopters would forgive everything for the core value.
- Confusing friends with early adopters. Friends encourage; early adopters *use*. The test is repeated behavior and referrals ([word of mouth](/t/word-of-mouth)), not enthusiasm.
- Abandoning them at scale. The features and quirks that served early adopters get "cleaned up" for the mainstream, alienating the base. Grow outward from them deliberately ([beachhead](/t/beachhead-market)).

**Convert them structurally:** turn the best into [design partners](/t/design-partners) with explicit expectations, then into reference customers and introductions. Early adopters are a go-to-market channel, not just a user group.
