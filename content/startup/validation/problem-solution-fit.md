**Problem-solution fit** is evidence — not hope — that your solution relieves a real problem for real people: they use the prototype repeatedly, pay or commit early, complain when it breaks, ask for more. It sits between finding a problem and finding a market: the problem is confirmed, the solution direction is confirmed, scale is not.

```text
no fit:       demo → "interesting!" → silence
fit:          rough prototype → repeated use → "when can we pay?" → pulling features from you
```

Look for pull over push. Users requesting access, tolerating bugs, working around missing pieces — demand dragging the product forward. If every user needs convincing, chasing and discounting, the fit is not there regardless of what interviews said.

The classic mistakes:

- Counting politeness as fit. Signups from friends, one curious call, a compliment — none of these survive contact with a price tag. Fit is measured in repeat behavior and money.
- Declaring fit from a concierge test and stopping. Hand-delivered service proves the problem; it does not prove a product can deliver it profitably. Fit must survive automation.
- Skipping to scale. Hiring sales and buying ads on problem-solution fit burns cash before [product-market fit](/t/product-market-fit) exists. Fit says "this direction"; PMF says "step on the gas".
- One customer as proof. A single enthusiastic design partner is a sample of one with selection bias. Fit needs a pattern across several independent customers.

**Next:** turn fit into a repeatable story — same problem, same solution, new customers converting without heroics. When strangers buy and stay without being convinced, you are leaving fit and entering [product-market fit](/t/product-market-fit).
