title: "Entering the Korean Market"
summary: "Selling in South Korea: local platforms, language and strong domestic competitors."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: scale }
---

Korea market entry is selling into one of the world's most connected, fastest-adopting digital markets — where consumers and enterprises switch quickly when value is real, and where domestic platform giants, strict privacy law and local business mechanics filter out foreign products that did not do their homework. The upside for well-localized software is unusually fast adoption; the tax on lazy entry is fast obscurity.

The structural notes (verify current rules with counsel — details shift):

```text
platforms:   KakaoTalk is the default communication layer (login,
             notifications, business messaging) and Naver anchors
             search; a product ignoring them undercounts its
             real reach
privacy:     PIPA is Korea's data-protection regime — strict,
             actively enforced, with local-representation-type
             obligations for some foreign operators
registration: certain business models (online content, commerce,
             sector services) carry local registration/licensing
             duties at scale thresholds
support:     enterprise buyers expect aggressive, real-time
             service culture; "email us" support is scored as
             neglect
capital:     a strong domestic VC and accelerator ecosystem,
             including government-backed programs (TIPS-style
             public-private support for startups)
```

The playbook mirrors [Japan](/t/japan-market-entry) with a different speed profile: local entity or partner for enterprise sales, Korean-language everything (sales, docs, support, app-store presence), and a named local lead who can navigate procurement relationships. The market punishes the "translate the landing page" approach far faster than Japan punishes it — Korean buyers move quickly but expect the same in service, and domestic challengers ship constantly.

The realistic read for SEA/foreign founders: Korea is a *premium niche* market — small geography, high density, brutal domestic competition in consumer categories, strong openings in B2B tooling where locals do not compete. Enter with one wedge segment and real support; do not enter as "also Korea" appended to a regional plan.
