**J-KISS** is Japan's standard early-stage instrument: convertible equity (technically structured around stock acquisition rights) functioning like a SAFE — money now, shares at the next priced round, with valuation cap and discount mechanics founders already understand. For seed rounds in Japan it is the default paperwork both sides expect.

```text
same shape as SAFE:  invest now → convert at priced round (cap/discount apply)
difference:          local legal form (stock acquisition rights), local counsel required
```

Use the standard forms unmodified where possible — the ecosystem's familiarity is the point. Custom terms slow Japanese processes (which prize precedent and correctness) disproportionately versus any conceivable economic gain.

The classic mistakes:

- Importing US SAFEs directly. Foreign forms in Japanese rounds confuse counsel, slow closings and misalign with local registration mechanics. Use J-KISS for Japan rounds; SAFEs where SAFEs are standard.
- Cap/discount imported unthinkingly. Valuation norms and round sizes differ by market; US-standard caps may misprice Japanese rounds structurally. Calibrate to local precedents with local counsel.
- Language-barrier signing. Bilingual documents where the Japanese version governs, reviewed only in English. Review the governing-language version with qualified counsel — always.
- Ignoring shareholder mechanics. Japanese corporate formalities (registrations, meetings, filings) run on rails unfamiliar to foreign founders. Local corporate secretary plus counsel from incorporation, not from the first problem.
- Assuming global investor fluency. Non-Japan investors encountering J-KISS need education; provide the SAFE-analogy memo proactively rather than discovering confusion mid-diligence.

**Default:** J-KISS on standard terms for Japanese seed rounds, local counsel throughout, and the same conversion-math discipline as SAFEs everywhere ([conversion math](/t/SAFE-conversion), [cap table](/t/cap-table)).
