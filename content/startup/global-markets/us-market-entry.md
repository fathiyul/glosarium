Entering the **US** offers the deepest budgets and fiercest competition in tech: customers pay more and churn faster, talent costs multiples, sales cycles reward professionalism, and every category already has funded incumbents. Win with focus (a wedge segment underserved by giants) rather than frontal assaults on entrenched leaders.

```text
entry kit:  US entity (often Delaware) + US payments/tax posture + reference customers
           + support hours coverage + pricing in USD without friction
```

Operationally: entity and banking first (customers and partners diligence these), USD pricing with US expectations (trials, SLAs, security reviews), support overlapping US hours, and sales motion matched to segment (PLG travels well; enterprise needs local presence eventually).

The classic mistakes:

- Premium pricing without premium proof. US buyers pay more but demand more — references, SLAs, compliance posture. Price confidence must be earned with evidence, not asserted with ambition.
- Understaffed trust functions. Security reviews, legal redlines and procurement questionnaires arrive immediately at B2B scale and sink unfunded teams for quarters. Staff solutions/architect roles ahead of pipeline, not behind it.
- Time-zone arrogance. "Async-first" covering a 12-hour gap with 24-hour response lags loses deals to local rivals' same-day turns. Coverage (follow-the-sun team or US hours overlap) is a feature buyers feel.
- State-by-state blindness. Sales tax nexus, employment rules and data laws vary by state. National launch without state mapping creates scattered liabilities discovered late.
- Marketing spend before motion. Pouring paid acquisition into an unproven US motion burns the expansion budget learning what focus groups would have taught. Prove motion small (design partners, lighthouse logos), then scale spend.

**Sequence:** entity + payments + 2–3 reference customers + support coverage → scale motion → hire locally. The US rewards focus and punishes tourism — enter to win a segment, not to plant a flag.
