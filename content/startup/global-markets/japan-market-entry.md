---
title: "Entering the Japanese Market"
summary: "Selling in Japan: trust, partners, localization and a long enterprise sales cycle."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: scale }
---

Japan market entry is selling into the world's fourth-or-fifth largest economy with a buying culture unlike anywhere else in tech: enormous value concentrated in a risk-averse, relationship-gated procurement world that punishes sloppy localization and rewards patient trust. The opportunity is real (stable spend, low churn once in, deep enterprise budgets); the discount rate is time.

What actually differentiates the market (patterns, not absolutes — verify specifics):

```text
buying:      long cycles, committee decisions, heavy vendor
             qualification; references and track record carry
             outsized weight; a foreign logo without a local
             case is a paperwork problem, not a pitch
language:    Japanese-first sales, contracts and support —
             English-native self-serve loses most enterprise
             deals by default
invoicing:   the qualified invoice regime (consumption-tax
             compliant invoicing, introduced from late 2023)
             changed what B2B billing must look like; register
             early, it gates customers' own deductions
trust:       partners (resellers, SIs, distributors) and local
             presence (an office, a country manager, even one
             credible Japanese-speaking AE) are the conversion
             infrastructure
ops:         meticulous service expectations — response time
             and correctness are scored harshly
```

The entity path mirrors [SEA](/t/regional-expansion-sea): representative office → local company ([KK vs GK](/t/japan-kk-vs-gk) is the incorporation choice) → hiring local staff with their own compliance regime. The classic mistakes: treating Japan as "one big English-speaking market with yen"; launching a product without Japanese documentation and expecting enterprise conversion; and staffing from San Francisco with a part-time bilingual contractor — the market reads the investment honestly.

When it is worth it: your product has measurable value against Japanese enterprise pain (efficiency, compliance, quality tooling), you can commit 2-3 years and a local nucleus, and you have patience the P&L can absorb. When it is not: as a top-of-deck TAM line, or as a growth move from a company that cannot yet fund its home market.
