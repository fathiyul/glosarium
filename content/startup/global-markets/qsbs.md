**QSBS (Qualified Small Business Stock)** lets US founders and early investors exclude large gains on qualifying startup stock held long enough (commonly five-plus years, with caps and conditions). Done right it transforms exit math — millions in gains untaxed that would otherwise face full capital rates. Done unclaimed (the usual case) it is the most expensive tax surprise in startup finance.

```text
qualify (C-corp + size limits + active business + holding period + original issuance...)
→ hold past the period → exclude gains up to applicable caps at exit
```

Qualification is fact-specific and fragile: entity type, asset thresholds, active-business tests, holding periods per lot, state conformity varying. Stacking, rollover and exclusion-cap mechanics reward planning years before exits — and punish discovery at closing, when restructuring is impossible.

The classic mistakes:

- Learning about QSBS at exit. Qualification conditions must hold for *years* beforehand. First conversation with tax counsel happens at formation/first financing, not at the acquisition offer.
- Assuming issuance qualifies. Secondary purchases, certain entity histories and non-original-issue paths fail tests founders never read. Verify each lot's status deliberately, documented.
- State blindness. Federal exclusion without state conformity still bills heavily in some states. Model combined federal-plus-state outcomes for founder locations actually involved.
- Holding-period resets unnoticed. Exchanges, conversions and restructurings can restart clocks silently. Every corporate action gets a QSBS-impact check before signing.
- Counting it in valuations prematurely. Buyers discount for *their* tax position, not yours; QSBS benefits sellers specifically. Valuable, real — and separate from company value.

**No caps, rates or thresholds quoted here** — they move with legislation. Learn the shape from this page, confirm current figures with US tax counsel early and often, and calendar holding periods per lot like the money they are.
