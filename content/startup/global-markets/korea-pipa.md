Korea's **PIPA (Personal Information Protection Act)** is among the world's stricter data regimes: narrow consent requirements, breach notification duties with real deadlines, and an regulator (PIPC) with active enforcement habits including public naming. Operating in Korea means treating data protection as a product requirement, not legal decoration.

```text
posture:  consent per purpose (bundled consents fail) + minimal collection
          + breach response clock running from discovery + cross-border care
```

Particular sharp edges: children's data rules, CCTV/location data sensitivities, marketing consent trails that must survive inspection, and breach notifications where delay itself becomes the violation. Documentation quality decides outcomes as much as practices do.

The classic mistakes:

- Global privacy template pasted on Korea. Overlapping-but-distinct duties (consent granularity, notification tempo, local specifics) need Korea-specific review. Templates localize; assumptions do not transfer.
- Marketing consent gaps. Campaigns, retargeting and messaging consents audited harshly when complaints arise — and complaints arise. Consent trails per channel, revocable, documented.
- Breach response improvised. Notification clocks start at discovery; improvised responses miss them. Playbook written, roles assigned, templates ready — before the incident.
- Employee monitoring overreach. Workplace tools collecting more than duties require trigger the same regime as customer data. Scope monitoring to necessity with notice.
- Assuming small-company invisibility. Enforcement samples across sizes; partnerships and app distribution surface practices regardless. Comply at entry scale.

**Pair with:** real [privacy practice](/t/privacy-policy), consent architecture per purpose, and local counsel review before processing begins. Compare [GDPR](/t/gdpr) for the European sibling most teams know first.
