Entering the **EU** means entering twenty-seven markets wearing a trench coat: one GDPR, but separate languages, VAT registrations (simplified somewhat by OSS schemes), consumer-protection regimes, employment rules and payment habits per country. "Europe" as a single launch is the first mistake; sequenced country entries with local readiness each is the playbook.

```text
sequence:  1–2 lead markets (English-friendly, digital-ready) → localize proof → expand stepwise
gates each: GDPR posture + VAT registration + language/support + payments + local entity/partner?
```

GDPR compliance is table stakes with real enforcement (see [GDPR](/t/gdpr)), VAT needs country-aware handling ([cross-border tax](/t/cross-border-digital-tax)), and B2B buyers expect data residency answers and DPAs as routine. None of this is exotic — it is simply more paperwork per revenue dollar than domestic growth, budgeted accordingly.

The classic mistakes:

- US playbook pasted onto Europe. Aggressive outbound, English-only, self-serve assumptions — underperforming against local norms of relationship selling, language and process. Localize motion, not just strings.
- GDPR theater. Cookie banners over unlawful data practices, DPAs unsigned, breach playbooks missing. Enforcement targets visible foreign startups disproportionately when complaints arise — comply genuinely.
- All-countries-at-once launch. Support load in twelve languages, VAT filings everywhere, zero density anywhere. Sequence 1–2 markets to repeatability first.
- Pricing uniformity. Purchasing power and willingness span widely (Nordics vs Southern vs Eastern Europe). Tier by market reality ([regional pricing](/t/regional-pricing)), not by headquarters convenience.
- Ignoring works councils and labor rigidity. Hiring (and especially firing) rules exceed US/SEA founder experience substantially. Local employment counsel before the first EU hire, not after the first dispute.

**Budget honestly:** EU expansion costs 2–3× the plan of founders who have only scaled domestically — in compliance, localization, support and patience. Enter deliberately, country by country, with local advice engaged before revenue depends on it.
