**TIPS (Tech Incubator Program for Startup)** pairs private angel/VC investment with matching government R&D funding: an accredited operator invests, the government multiplies it into research money. For eligible tech startups it meaningfully extends runway without dilution — private validation unlocking public capital on top.

```text
shape:  private lead invests → government matches multiples into R&D → milestones + reporting
fit:    tech/R&D-heavy startups with Korean presence and credible private backing
```

Access runs through accredited operators and angels — the private lead is the gate, not the application portal. Program specifics (match ratios, caps, eligible uses, operator lists) move with budgets; confirm current terms rather than relying on any guide's numbers.

The classic mistakes:

- Chasing TIPS before private backing. The match needs something to match — credible lead investors first, program second. Grants follow traction and validation, never precede them.
- R&D theater to fit criteria. Bending the roadmap toward fundable keywords distorts the company for one-time money. Fit must be genuine or the milestones become lies with auditors.
- Underestimating reporting load. Matched public money brings milestone evidence, audits and usage constraints. Price the administrative overhead against the check honestly.
- Ignoring presence requirements. Korean entity, activity and substance expectations accompany the money. Paper presence without operations fails diligence sooner or later.
- One-program dependence. Policy programs cycle with budgets and politics. Diversify funding like everything else — TIPS accelerates a funded plan, it is not the plan.

**Use it as:** non-dilutive R&D extension on top of a privately-validated plan, accessed through credible operators, with eyes open on obligations. See [non-dilutive funding](/t/non-dilutive-funding) for the global playbook.
