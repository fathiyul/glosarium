Japan offers two main company forms: the **KK (kabushiki kaisha)** — stock company with shareholders, directors and formal governance, the standard for venture-backed startups — and the **GK (godo kaisha)** — membership company with simpler governance, cheaper setup, and flexibility that suits subsidiaries and small businesses. Venture investors expect KK; cost-conscious operators often prefer GK.

```text
KK:  stock structure familiar to investors · formal governance · higher setup/running cost
GK:  flexible, cheaper, simpler · less familiar to VCs · fine for subsidiaries/small ops
```

Foreign founders add layers: visa status tied to business-manager requirements (capital thresholds, office, staff plans), bank accounts requiring in-person process, and the hanko/seal culture persisting beside digital signatures. None block entry; all punish improvisation.

The classic mistakes:

- GK for a venture path. Then converting (or explaining) at every fundraise. If venture money is even possible, KK from the start — conversion mid-journey costs more than either initial choice.
- Visa-structure mismatch. Company form, capital, office and hiring must satisfy immigration together. Incorporate and immigrate as one plan with one advisor team, never separately.
- Underestimating setup friction. Timelines, notarizations, translations and capital-verification steps run slower than SEA/US founder experience suggests. Start months before needed, with local judicial scrivener (shiho-shoshi) support.
- Governance theater. KK formalities (meetings, minutes, filings) observed loosely "because we're a startup" — then fatal in diligence or disputes. Small-company discipline from day one.
- Tax blindness across borders. Japanese corporate, consumption and withholding taxes interacting with home-country regimes need coordinated advice early, especially for foreign founders and remote teams.

**Choose KK for venture-track, GK for lean local ops** — with immigration, banking and tax planned as one package with local professionals. See [Japan market entry](/t/japan-market-entry) for the commercial half.
