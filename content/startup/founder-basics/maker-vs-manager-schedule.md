The **maker vs manager schedule** (Paul Graham's distinction) says makers need half-day unbroken blocks while managers live in hourly meetings — and founders live in both, badly, unless they split deliberately. An hour-long meeting in a maker's morning destroys the whole morning, not just the hour; context, not time, is what shatters.

```text
maker mode:   4-hour blocks, no meetings, notifications off → deep work (code, strategy, writing)
manager mode: batched meetings, office hours, quick decisions → people work in bursts
```

Run the week in modes, not mush: makers' mornings (or full maker days Tuesday/Thursday), meeting batches afternoons, one fully clear day if possible. As the company grows the manager share grows inexorably — protect maker time explicitly or watch it vanish by default.

The classic mistakes:

- Uniform availability. Open calendar, instant replies, meetings scattered hourly — maximum responsiveness, zero deep work. Availability is a resource to budget, not a virtue to maximize.
- Maker guilt. Feeling unproductive during thinking/writing blocks because "nothing shipped". Deep work's output is invisible daily and decisive quarterly — defend it with outcomes, not activity logs.
- Manager work done badly in maker scraps. 1:1s squeezed between coding sprints get distracted halves of a mind. Batch people work with full presence or do not do it.
- No transition ritual. Context-switching hourly between modes without reset burns the worst of both. Buffers between modes (walk, notes, shutdown ritual) aren't luxury, they're throughput.
- Imposing your schedule on makers. Founder-manager rhythm (meetings all day!) forced on engineers destroys their output while feeling collaborative. Asymmetric schedules are correct — defend makers' calendars harder than your own.

**The rule:** makers get blocks, managers get batches, founders get both by design — declared, defended weekly. See [delegation](/t/delegation) for shrinking the manager load itself.
