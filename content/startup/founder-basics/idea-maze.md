The **idea maze** is the map of everything around your idea: who tried what, which paths dead-ended and why, where the bodies are buried, and which adjacent attempts suggest a live route. Before committing years, spend weeks learning the maze — every failed predecessor paid tuition you can collect free.

```text
maze map:  attempt A (2019, died: CAC too high) → attempt B (pivot to enterprise, alive)
           your entry: same wedge as A? different timing? different customer?
```

Research it like an analyst: dead startups' post-mortems, failed features inside big companies, academic work, adjacent markets where the same idea worked. Patterns in the failures are the actual data — "everyone dies at onboarding" tells you where your innovation must live.

The classic mistakes:

- Skipping the maze from overconfidence. "Nobody has done this" usually means nobody you asked. Assume three teams tried; find them before they find you in the market.
- Treating dead companies as proof it cannot work. Corpses teach constraints, not verdicts — timing, team, pricing and distribution differ. Learn the cause of death, not just the fact.
- Mapping competitors but not substitutes. The maze includes spreadsheets, manual work, agencies and apathy — the paths customers actually take today (see [competitor analysis](/t/competitor-analysis)).
- Analysis paralysis. A month of mapping beats a year of blind building; three months of mapping is fear. Timebox it, then enter with eyes open.
