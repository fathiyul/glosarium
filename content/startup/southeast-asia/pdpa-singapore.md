Singapore's **PDPA (Personal Data Protection Act)** governs collection, use, disclosure and care of personal data: consent (deemed in more situations than founders expect), purpose limitation, protection obligations, access/correction rights, and mandatory breach notification past thresholds. Enforcement is active and fines are real — this is complied-with law, not decorative text.

```text
duties:  consent (or valid exception) + purpose limits + protection + access/correction
         + breach notification + DPO appointment + cross-border care
```

For startups the sharp edges are breach notification (assess fast, notify when thresholds trigger — rehearse the playbook, don't discover it mid-incident), marketing consent (telemarketing and bulk messages need clear consent trails), and employee data (often forgotten until a dispute).

The classic mistakes:

- Assuming EU/GDPR compliance transfers. Overlapping principles, different specifics — deemed consent, DNC registry rules and local enforcement practice need Singapore-specific review.
- No data protection officer. Appointing a DPO (even a part-time or outsourced one) is required and checked; "the CTO handles privacy" without designation fails the letter and the spirit.
- Breach discovered without a plan. No playbook means improvising notification decisions under the clock. Write down who decides, who notifies and with what templates before the incident.
- Vendor data sprawling uncontracted. Analytics, support and cloud tools processing user data need [DPAs](/t/data-processing-agreement) and transfer assessments — the startup's responsibility, not the vendor's.
- Employee and applicant data ignored. Resumes, monitoring tools, references — personal data with the same duties. Cover the whole lifecycle, not just customers.

**Pair with:** a real [privacy policy](/t/privacy-policy), consent flows that match the law (not dark patterns), and counsel review before regional expansion multiplies the regimes. Compare [Malaysia's PDPA](/t/pdpa-malaysia) and Indonesia's [UU PDP](/t/uu-pdp) where you operate across borders.
