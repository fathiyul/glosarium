Southeast Asia's country differences are the reason regional strategy exists: six markets a founder can fly across in a day behave like different continents in payments, platforms, language, regulation and buying culture. The map below is directional (generalizations hold until they don't — verify each market's current rules), but knowing the axes beats rediscovering them through a failed launch.

```text
Indonesia  largest population/economy; Jakarta-concentrated;
           Gojek/Grab-era super-app habits; COD + e-wallet +
           bank-transfer payment mix; active local-content and
           licensing regimes; UU PDP data law
Singapore  small market, regional HQ gravity: banking, legal,
           holding structures; English-first, card-heavy, high
           cost per hire
Malaysia   middle-income, English widely used in business;
           Sdn Bhd incorporation; SST; PDPA; halal dynamics
           matter for consumer goods; KL as cheaper regional base
Thailand   Bangkok-dominated; LINE as business messaging spine;
           promptpay rails; foreign-ownership rules by sector
Vietnam    fast developer base and strong domestic platforms
           (messaging, e-wallets); data-localization instincts;
           sector-specific foreign-ownership limits
Philippines English-first, mobile-first, remittance economy;
           GCash-led wallets; archipelago logistics cost;
           light-touch but patchy regulation
```

The practical reads: payment rails decide your checkout design per country (what converts in Manila confuses in Jakarta); platform ecosystems decide your distribution (what an app does on [QRIS](/t/qris) Indonesia rails differs from Vietnam's domestic-app gravity); regulatory texture decides your entity plan ([pt-pma](/t/pt-pma)-style local structures in Indonesia versus wholly-owned in Singapore); language depth decides your support stack (English works in PH/SG, barely opens B2B doors in ID/VN/TH mass markets).

The expansion mistake: averaging. A "SEA-wide" product usually means a product that fits none of them well — the companies that compound locally (localized rails, local team, local compliance) own the market others fly into. See [regional expansion](/t/regional-expansion-sea) for sequencing, and the country-specific pages for the regulatory corners.
