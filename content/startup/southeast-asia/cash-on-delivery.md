**Cash on delivery (COD)** — pay cash (or transfer on the spot) when goods arrive — remains common in parts of Southeast Asia for e-commerce, especially outside major cities and with first-time online buyers. It converts the unbanked and the distrustful, at the cost of failed deliveries, handling overhead and reconciliation complexity that prepaid rails avoid.

```text
conversion:  higher (no payment friction, no trust needed upfront)
costs:       failed-delivery returns + cash handling + slower cycles + reconciliation drag
```

Offer it where it moves the needle (new-buyer segments, low-trust categories, outer cities) and measure ruthlessly: return rates, successful-collection rates and fully-loaded cost per COD order versus prepaid. Many sellers find a hybrid optimum — COD for first orders converting to prepaid wallets thereafter.

The classic mistakes:

- COD everywhere by default. Blanket availability maximizes failed deliveries and cash drag. Gate by segment, order value and buyer history — earn prepaid migration with incentives.
- No failed-delivery process. Returns piling in warehouses unprocessed while refunds lag destroy margins quietly. Reverse logistics needs the same rigor as forward.
- Fake orders unhandled. COD's low friction invites prank and competitor orders. Confirmation steps (call/SMS verification for large or suspicious orders) pay for themselves immediately.
- Reconciliation chaos. Cash across couriers, partial payments, damaged returns — without per-order tracking, leakage becomes structural. Reconcile per order, per courier, on schedule.
- Keeping it past its usefulness. As trust and digital payments grow in a segment, COD's costs persist while its conversion edge fades. Sunset per segment with data, not nostalgia.

**Treat COD as:** a conversion tool with measurable cost, optimized per segment — not a default, not a relic. See [payment methods](/t/indonesia-payment-methods) for the rail mix it sits within.
