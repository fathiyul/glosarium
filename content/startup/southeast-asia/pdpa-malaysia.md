Malaysia's PDPA — the Personal Data Protection Act 2010, in force since 2013 and amended recently to strengthen it — is the country's general data protection law. It governs how companies process personal data in commercial transactions: consent, purpose, notice, security, retention, access, and conditions on keeping data in or moving it out of Malaysia. For founders operating [regionally](/t/regional-expansion-sea), it is the Malaysian layer of the same discipline that [UU PDP](/t/uu-pdp) and Singapore's [PDPA](/t/pdpa-singapore) impose next door — similar family, different details.

The obligations that actually shape product work:

- **Notice and consent** — a privacy policy users can find, and lawful basis for processing; sensitive data carries stricter requirements.
- **Security and retention** — reasonable protective measures; keep data only as long as the purpose needs; disposal duties.
- **Disclosure and cross-border** — sharing with third parties and transferring outside Malaysia are conditioned (the amendment era moved some of this toward more permissive-but-documented frameworks; verify current rules).
- **Enforcement** — a dedicated commissioner, with recent amendments adding breach-notification duties and materially raised penalties.

The classic mistakes: copying the EU GDPR playbook and assuming it auto-complies everywhere (PDPA is simpler in structure, stricter in some corners like sensitive data and cross-border mechanics); treating marketing consent as a checkbox after the first complaint; and leaving the compliance line out of vendor contracts ([DPA-style terms](/t/data-processing-agreement) for processors).

For the founder deciding architecture: build to the *strictest plausible* SEA requirement (policies, retention, deletion, consent logs), and country-specific mapping becomes a checklist instead of a rebuild. Confirm current Malaysian requirements with local counsel — the amendment implementation details have been moving through recent phases.
