Regional expansion in Southeast Asia is the disciplined move from "we win one country" to "we replicate in several" — and in SEA the replication is harder than it looks because the region is not a market, it is six-plus markets that share a time zone. The planning frame: pick the next country by *fit and distance*, set up the legal/financial spine before the first local sale, and staff a real local owner, not a remote calendar.

The spine decisions, roughly in order:

```text
1 entity & holding:  which structure owns the region — the common
   answer is a parent (Singapore is the frequent pick for tax,
   banking and law) plus operating subsidiaries; see
   singapore-holding-structure, pt-pma, malaysia-sdn-bhd
2 order:             the next market closest to your proven one —
   language, payment rails, buying behavior, regulatory style
   (the country comparison page covers the differences)
3 money & tax:       cross-border billing, VAT/GST and withholding
   per country (cross-border-digital-tax), transfer pricing on
   intra-group services
4 people:            local hires on local payroll (BPJS-style
   obligations per country), foreign-owner/expat permits only
   where the skill genuinely is not local
5 data:              per-country privacy law (uu-pdp,
   pdpa-malaysia, pdpa-singapore) and hosting/transfer posture
```

The classic mistakes: regionalizing too early (a #2 market team funded by a #1 market that has not proven repeatable economics — expansion is the most expensive way to discover you needed product work), cloning go-to-market instead of adapting it (what sells in Jakarta sells differently in Bangkok and Manila — the [super-app and platform gravity](/t/sea-country-differences) differ), and doing the [holding structure](/t/delaware-flip) as tax cleanup after revenue exists instead of before it (flipping mid-expansion means renegotiating every local entity).

The honest test before launching country two: can you name the local first-year economics — expected [CAC](/t/cac), pricing after local tax, the compliance bill, and the person who will run it — on one page you could defend at a board meeting? If the page is vague, the expansion is a bet with a travel budget attached. When it is genuinely right: home-market ceiling is real, the product's value is portable, and the spine decisions above are no longer open questions.
