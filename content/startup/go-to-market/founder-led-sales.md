**Founder-led sales** means founders personally sell until the motion is understood and repeatable: you run the calls, hear objections raw, iterate the pitch weekly, and learn what actually closes. Nobody can delegate discovery — the sales playbook must be written by someone who felt every objection firsthand.

```text
founder phase:  you sell → learn objections → sharpen pitch → close first 10–20
handover:       playbook exists → hire sellers → you coach → you leave calls entirely
```

Founders close through credibility and flexibility no hired rep matches early: you can promise roadmap, adjust terms, and speak with builder authority. That advantage is temporary and must be converted into process before it becomes dependency.

The classic mistakes:

- Hiring sales before selling. A sales hire with no playbook, no proof and no coaching burns salary learning what founders should have learned free. Sell first; hire into motion, not into fog.
- Never leaving calls. At some size the founder on every call caps throughput and infantilizes the team. Define the handover trigger (repeatable close rate without you) and honor it.
- Discounting like a founder. Founder authority lets prospects extract promises and prices no rep should match. Sell value even while learning — desperation discounts train the market.
- Confusing activity with motion. Fifty calls with no evolving pitch is motion. Track objection patterns and pitch changes weekly; motion without learning is just calendar burn.
- Skipping the CRM from day one. "Too early for process" produces lost follow-ups and invisible pipelines. Log everything from call one — future sales ops will worship you (see [sales pipeline](/t/sales-pipeline)).

**Hand over when:** strangers close at predictable rates following your documented playbook, and your calendar, not your presence, is the constraint. Then hire ([first sales hire](/t/first-sales-hire)) and get back to building the company only founders can build.
