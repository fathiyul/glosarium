title: "Sales Commission"
summary: "Paying salespeople: base salary, variable pay, accelerators and clawbacks."

Sales commission pays sellers part base, part variable, tied to the results you actually want. The plan is a behavior machine: people optimize the metric you pay on, so the design question is not "what's a fair percentage" but "which actions produce [revenue](/t/revenue-recognition) that stays."

Common shapes: a base for stability, variable on bookings or collected revenue, accelerators above quota, and clawbacks on churned or reversed deals. The base/variable mix depends on cycle length — heavier base where deals take quarters to close, leaner where the motion is high-velocity; every market and role differs, so anchor your plan to what comparable sellers earn locally rather than a copied template.

```text
pay on what stays:     collected cash, activated logos, retained ARR
accidental payments:   signed bookings that never onboard,
                       quarterly deals cancelled at renewal
```

The failure modes are specific:

- **Paying on bookings.** Reps close bad-fit customers, collect commission, and leave before [churn](/t/churn-rate) shows the cost. Pay partly on activation or cash collected; keep clawbacks real and enforce them once so the policy is believed.
- **Complexity.** Plans nobody can calculate in their head don't motivate — they generate support tickets to finance.
- **One-size quotas.** Ramp periods for new hires (reduced quota for the first stretch is common practice), and territory/account-value differences that make quota a lottery distort behavior more than any accelerators fix.
- **Founder exceptions.** Overriding the plan for your own back-channel deals teaches the whole team the plan is advisory.

When not to complicate: small teams and founder-led sales — a flat percentage or a bonus pool does the job until volume justifies structure. And during land-grab phases where growth matters more than efficiency, generous accelerators buy speed you might otherwise not get — expensive, deliberate, valid.
