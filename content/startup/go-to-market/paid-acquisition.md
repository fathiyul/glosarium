title: "Paid Acquisition"
summary: "Buying users through ads, and when the unit economics allow it."

---

Paid acquisition buys customers with advertising: search, social, programmatic, sponsorships. It is the fastest scalable demand source most companies will ever have — and the fastest way to burn money if run without [unit economics](/t/unit-economics) discipline. The entire business case in one sentence: marginal cost per acquired customer must sit below the value customers bring, *on an incremental basis*.

The traps are measurement-shaped:

- **Attribution ≠ incrementality.** Platforms claim every signup they touched. The only honest test is a holdout — spend paused in one region or a 10% budget cut with conversion watched — because the "return" on the last dollar of spend is not the return on the first ([CAC payback](/t/cac-payback-period) at scale, not at the average).
- **Blended CAC hides the marginal.** A $30 blended CAC can sit next to a $90 marginal cost at your current budget level; that's what "doubling spend halved efficiency" looks like.
- **Creative fatigue is real.** Performance decays as audiences saturate; new creative and channels restart the curve. Paid is a treadmill you cannot step off if it carries growth.

The scaling curve has a shape: every channel has a ceiling where audiences exhaust, bids rise and returns collapse. Expanding spend past that ceiling doesn't fail — it works *more expensively*, which is worse until you notice the [burn multiple](/t/burn-multiple) drifting.

When paid is right: you know the LTV of a customer from real cohorts, [conversion](/t/pricing-page) and activation are measurable, the loop closes on cash within a horizon the [runway](/t/runway) funds, and organic/word-of-mouth are complements not hopes. When it is wrong: pre-PMF (paying rent for a product nobody retains), unmeasurable value chains (enterprise funnels where a demo request costs real but closes in quarters), or as a substitute for retention — buying leaky buckets is the most reliable growth strategy in the graveyard.
