**Cold outreach** is contacting prospects with no prior relationship — email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp where business runs on it — to start sales conversations. Done well it is the fastest predictable pipeline for B2B: targeted lists, personal relevance, clear ask. Done badly it is spam that burns domains, reputations and inboxes.

```text
good:  50 precise prospects → researched lines → one clear ask → 10 replies → 3 meetings
spam:  5,000 scraped addresses → generic pitch → bounces, blocks, domain blacklisted
```

The craft is relevance at scale: triggers (funding news, hiring posts, tech changes), oneobservation openers proving homework, and asks so small they are easier to accept than ignore. Volume matters less than hit rate — fifty researched emails beat five thousand blasts on every metric including deliverability.

The classic mistakes:

- Volume over relevance. Spray-and-pray destroys sender reputation ([deliverability](/t/email-deliverability)) and teaches nothing. If reply rates sit near zero, the list or message is wrong — fix, don't scale.
- Pitching in message one. A novel-length product dump to strangers converts nobody. Earn the reply first (one insight + one question), pitch on the call.
- No follow-up sequence. Most replies come on touches 2–4, but most founders stop at one. Plan 3–5 touches adding value each time; stop at explicit no.
- Illegal or rule-breaking outreach. Unsolicited commercial email rules, platform spam policies and data laws (Indonesia's [UU PDP](/t/uu-pdp), EU GDPR) apply to founders too. Comply, offer opt-outs, respect them instantly.
- Outsourcing before understanding. Agencies burning your domain on bad lists while you learn nothing. Run it personally until reply rates stabilize, then systematize or delegate.

**Measure:** reply rate (target double digits on researched lists), positive rate, meetings booked — never sent count. Outreach is a conversion funnel like any other; manage it as one.
