The **sales cycle** is the duration and step sequence from first touch to signed contract — weeks for SMB self-serve, quarters for enterprise, with pilots, security reviews and procurement as the long poles. Knowing your cycle length (measured, not hoped) sets everything: pipeline coverage needed, cash planning, hiring pace, fundraise timing.

```text
measure:  first touch → close dates across won deals (median + distribution, by segment)
use:      pipeline coverage = quota ÷ win rate, timed by cycle length ahead of need
```

Shorten structurally, not by pressure: multi-threading early (no single-thread stalls), parallel security/legal tracks, mutual action plans with buyers, and disqualification fast (bad-fit deals consume cycles that good ones need).

The classic mistakes:

- Planning on best-case cycles. The fastest deal ever becomes the forecast template; reality runs 2–3× longer. Plan on medians, staff for distributions.
- Ignoring segment variance. SMB 3-week and enterprise 9-month cycles averaged into a meaningless "4 months". Measure and plan per segment — they are different businesses operationally.
- Procurement discovered at signature. Legal, security and vendor onboarding surfacing after verbal yes add surprise quarters. Map buying process (not just champion enthusiasm) at qualification.
- Founder-cycle math for team planning. Founder-closed speed (credibility + authority shortcuts) does not transfer to new reps. New-hire ramps and team cycles run longer — model theirs, not yours.
- No cycle-shortening program. Accepting length as weather. Attack structurally: templates, parallel tracks, mutual plans, product-led trial paths that pre-sell before sales engages.

**Track monthly** by segment: median length, stage durations, stall reasons. Cycle length is the constraint that schedules revenue — manage it as deliberately as pipeline volume.
