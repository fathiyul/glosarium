**Community-led growth** grows through users who help each other: answering questions, writing guides, recruiting peers, improving the product's reputation by association. The community becomes support (deflecting tickets), marketing (authentic advocacy), product research (unfiltered needs) and moat (belonging competitors cannot copy) at once.

```text
seed:   founders present daily → early members helped visibly → norms set (helpfulness, no spam)
compound: members answer faster than staff → content library grows → newcomers onboard via peers
```

It works for products with learning curves, craft pride or shared identity — dev tools, creator platforms, niche professional software. Pure utilities with no identity layer struggle; nobody forms a community around a password reset flow.

The classic mistakes:

- Company-controlled "community" (a broadcast channel with comments off). Communities need member-to-member value, not brand content distribution. If members gain nothing from each other, it is marketing with extra steps.
- Premature scaling. Hiring community managers and buying platform software for twelve members. Founders seed personally until hundreds active; process follows proof.
- Neglecting norms early. Spam, self-promotion and toxicity set the culture in weeks. Moderate firmly from day one — the first hundred members define the next ten thousand.
- Extracting without giving. Mining the community for testimonials and upsells while contributing nothing back burns the exact trust growth needs. Give status, access, influence and genuine help first.
- Measuring members, not aliveness. Ten thousand registered with twelve talking is a mausoleum. Track weekly contributors, answer speed, member-to-member help rate — aliveness metrics, not headcount.

**Founder involvement** never fully delegates: the community needs to feel the company's presence, especially early. Show up daily until culture runs itself, then stay visibly around. See [super apps](/t/super-apps) for the scaled extreme of user ecosystems (different game, same principle: users attracting users).
