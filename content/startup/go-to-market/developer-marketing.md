**Developer marketing** reaches developers — who choose tools bottom-up and distrust traditional marketing — through documentation quality, open source, honest technical content and community presence. Developers adopt what works, recommend what delights, and punish condescension permanently. Marketing to them means being genuinely good plus genuinely visible.

```text
channels that work:  great docs + quickstarts · open source · technical blog posts with code
                     Hacker News/Reddit presence · conference talks · changelog discipline
channels that backfire: banner ads · buzzword decks · gated whitepapers · growth-hacked spam
```

Credibility is the currency and engineers mint it slowly: answer questions helpfully for months before asking anything, open-source real tools (not demos), write posts with working code, and let engineers (not marketers) be the voice wherever possible.

The classic mistakes:

- Marketing-speak to engineers. "Synergistic AI-powered platform" triggers instant dismissal. Plain technical language, benchmarks, architecture honesty — respect the audience's intelligence or lose it forever.
- Gated everything. Email walls before docs filter exactly the evaluators you want (busy skeptics bounce; only the desperate persist). Open docs convert; gates leak.
- Community extraction. Taking answers and attention without giving (no contributions, no presence, drive-by launches). Communities have long memories for takers — contribute quarters before asking days.
- Ignoring DX as marketing. Slow signup, broken quickstart, dead example repos — every friction point is anti-marketing visible in public. Developer experience *is* the top of funnel ([API product](/t/api-as-a-product)).
- Vanity DevRel metrics. Talk views and Twitter followers while trials flatline. Measure downstream: quickstart completions, integration starts, community-to-customer conversion.

**Staff it with engineers** who write and speak, not marketers learning tech. Authenticity cannot be outsourced — developers smell it instantly. See [open core](/t/open-core) for the business model this marketing most often serves.
