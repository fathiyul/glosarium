---
title: "Expansion Revenue"
summary: "Growing revenue from existing customers through upsell, cross-sell and more seats."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Expansion revenue is new revenue from existing customers — more seats, higher tiers, added modules, deeper usage. It is the growth engine hiding inside the accounts you already have, and it is why [NRR](/t/net-revenue-retention) above 100% makes a company compound: expansion is the term that does it.

The mechanics that work:

```text
triggers:  a team grows, usage hits tier limits, new use case appears
           from a different department, the exec sponsor changes
           with a growth mandate
signals:   seat requests, API growth, sandbox usage, support topics
           from a department you've never sold
motion:    CS or AE owns the account plan and asks at the right moment;
           product nudges own the self-serve moment (tier banner,
           usage warning, one-click add)
```

The [product-led](/t/product-led-growth) version is cleanest: [usage-based pricing](/t/usage-based-pricing) or seat tiers that expand without a meeting — no one waits for a QBR to buy more storage. For complex accounts, expansion needs a *person*: someone knows that the other division started using the trial, and someone turns it into a plan.

The classic mistakes: counting expansion revenue from deals that should have been priced properly at the start (a discount recovered is not expansion); pushing expansion as a quota pressure tactic — upselling before the customer has value manufactures churn later; and measuring only at company level, hiding that expansion comes from five logos while 200 stall ([concentration risk](/t/logo-churn-vs-revenue-churn) wearing a growth badge).

The tradeoff with churn: aggressive expansion metrics can push CS to pitch instead of fix. Keep health before upsell in the playbook — expansion off a degraded account accelerates the exit.

When not to rely on it: low-price SMB products where account value is too small for expansion conversations to pencil, or products where the customer's use genuinely tops out at tier one — then expansion is a rounding error and [new logos](/t/cac-payback-period) decide everything.
