**Content marketing** attracts customers by publishing genuinely useful material — guides that rank on search, tools that solve micro-problems, videos that teach, research the industry cites. Prospects arrive with trust pre-built, having already received value; sales conversations start warm instead of cold.

```text
compounding asset:  100 guides ranking → steady qualified traffic → trials → customers
vs ads:             spend → traffic → stop spending → silence (rented, not owned)
```

It compounds where ads rent: each piece works for years, ranks higher with age and links, and builds domain authority competitors must outwait rather than outspend. The price is patience — meaningful traffic takes quarters, and quitting at month three wastes the investment exactly before payoff.

The classic mistakes:

- Content about the company, not the customer. Launch announcements and culture posts that buyers never search for. Write for buyer problems and search intent, not the company blog aesthetic.
- Inconsistency. Three posts then silence for months. Search and audience compound on cadence — weekly beats brilliant-but-rare. Commit to a sustainable rhythm or do not start.
- No conversion path. Thousands of readers, zero trials — content without CTAs, product tie-ins and email capture is philanthropy. Every piece routes somewhere measurable.
- AI-slop scaling. Mass-generated thin content that search engines demote and readers distrust. One genuinely useful piece outperforms fifty generated ones; quality compounds, spam decays.
- Attributing nothing. Content's long lag makes ROI arguments hard, so budgets die first. Track assisted conversions, rank trends and email growth from month one — imperfect attribution beats none.

**Pair with:** [SEO](/t/seo) fundamentals (the discovery half) and a newsletter capturing the audience algorithms cannot take away. Content is slow, then sudden — survive the slow part deliberately.
