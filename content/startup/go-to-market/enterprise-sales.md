**Enterprise sales** means selling to large organizations through committees, not individuals: champions, economic buyers, security reviewers, legal, procurement — each with vetoes, timelines and demands. Cycles run quarters; deals fund years. It is the highest-value, slowest, most process-intensive motion in B2B.

```text
motion:  qualify (real pain + budget + timeline?) → multi-thread (champion + buyer + users)
        → pilot proving value → security/legal gauntlet → procurement → close → expand
```

Founder-led enterprise works until volume demands a team: founders borrow credibility and cut through process by authority. The handover ([first sales hire](/t/first-sales-hire)) needs playbooks built from founder-won deals — process extracted from practice, never invented in a hiring doc.

The classic mistakes:

- Consumer timelines assumed. Quarterly closes planned monthly; runway math built on hope. Enterprise cycles average multiple quarters — plan cash and morale accordingly.
- Single-threaded deals. One champion, no economic-buyer access, surprise veto at signature. Multi-thread from pilot stage; no single throat to choke (yours).
- Security review ambush. Questionnaires, pen tests and DPAs arriving post-verbal-yes stall closes for quarters. Pre-build the trust pack ([security baseline](/t/startup-security-baseline), [DPA](/t/data-processing-agreement), architecture docs) and start reviews in parallel with pilots.
- Pilots without conversion mechanics. Free Enterprise trials drifting without success criteria or economic-buyer sponsorship. [Paid pilots](/t/paid-pilot) with conversion terms, always.
- Discounting to logo-collect. Flagship logos at ruinous prices and terms, celebrated internally, regretted for years. Price for sustainability; logos follow value, not discounts.

**Earn enterprise:** reference customers in their industry, compliance posture ready, pilots that prove ROI in their numbers, and patience funded by runway. Land few, expand deep, repeat deliberately.
