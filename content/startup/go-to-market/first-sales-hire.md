The **first sales hire** takes over a motion founders proved — not discovers one. Hire when founder-led deals close repeatably from a documented playbook, pipeline exceeds founder bandwidth, and the constraint is clearly selling time (not product gaps or missing proof). Earlier hires burn salary learning what founders should have learned free.

```text
ready signals:  10+ founder-closed deals · documented playbook · pipeline overflowing founder hours
hire profile:   player-coach seller (does + builds), startup-experienced,hungry over polished
```

Hire a player-coach, not a VP: someone who sells personally for quarters while building process, then grows into leadership if earned. Big-company sales leaders without startup selling experience fail here reliably — process knowledge without prospecting hunger starves in year one.

The classic mistakes:

- Hiring the resume, not the motion. Enterprise VP for a PLG motion (or reverse) — impressive logo, zero fit, expensive year. Match the hire's last three years to your next eighteen months exactly.
- No playbook to hand over. "Go figure out sales" as onboarding. The hire refines a working motion; they cannot invent one from founder anecdotes. Document first, hire second.
- Over-titling early. VP titles for individual contributors inflate comp, block future leadership hires, and set wrong expectations. Title for the job's next two years, with growth paths explicit.
- Commission plans that fight strategy. Paying new logos while needing expansion (or reverse) manufactures exactly the wrong behavior. Comp mirrors strategy, reviewed as strategy shifts.
- Abandoning selling founders entirely. Founders leaving all customer contact lose market feel within quarters. Keep key-account and strategic-deal involvement permanently — just not the whole pipeline.

**Onboard with:** ride-alongs on live deals, playbook study, then supervised closes with feedback — and quota ramps honoring the learning curve. The first sales hire either systematizes founder magic or exposes its absence; hire only when there is magic to systematize.
