title: "Channel Partners"
summary: "Resellers, agencies and system integrators who sell your product for a share of revenue."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Channel partners sell your product through their relationships: resellers, agencies, system integrators, marketplaces. The pitch is leverage — their customers already trust them, and one partner can produce pipeline you'd spend years building. The reality is a second sales motion with its own economics, training, conflict rules and a brutal filter: partners add you when selling you beats the alternative they already sell.

The economics first: partner margin (say 15-30% off list) must leave *your* [contribution margin](/t/contribution-margin) healthy after their support cost — channel is not free distribution, it is buying reach. Then the partner's math: their realized revenue per hour selling you versus their incumbent vendors. A young product loses that comparison unless you give them a wedge: exclusivity on a niche, a better product for deals incumbents fumble, services attach, or marketing funds.

The classic failures:

- **No partner motion.** Recruiting 40 partners with a one-page margin sheet and wondering why nothing happens. Enablement (training, demos, deal registration, co-marketing) is the product you sell *them*.
- **Channel conflict.** Your direct [sales team](/t/sales-led-growth) quoting the same account the next day; partners learn and stop registering deals. Deal-registration rules, enforced, are the whole game.
- **Partner-sourced ≠ partner-serviced.** Letting a reseller sell a deployment their team can't support turns your logo into their churn. Define who owns delivery and support per tier.

Marketplaces compress control further: great for discovery, thin margins, opaque ranking, and the platform can change terms whenever ([take-rate](/t/take-rate) economics and [platform risk](/t/platform-risk) apply to software distribution too).

When channel is right: the product needs local hands (implementation, hardware, services), buyers trust integrators over cold vendors ([SEA enterprise buying](/t/sea-country-differences) often does), or your direct CAC is prohibitive. When it's wrong: pre-PMF (nobody can sell what doesn't churn-check), or when partners will capture the customer relationship you need for subscription revenue.
