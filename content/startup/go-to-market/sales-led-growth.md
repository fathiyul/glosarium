---
title: "Sales-Led Growth"
summary: "Growing through a sales team, typically for larger and more complex deals."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Sales-led growth means a team of sellers drives new customers: outbound, demos, negotiation, contracts. It is the right motion when value is big, complex, or organizationally hard to buy alone — enterprise products where no one person can decide, where onboarding needs hands, where the price is negotiated not published.

The cost structure decides everything: sellers are expensive and slow to ramp, so sales-led requires prices high enough to carry them. Rough arithmetic founders use: a fully loaded seller (salary, benefits, tools, ramp) costs a substantial fixed number per year that varies widely by market; the territory must produce several multiples of that in gross profit, and the buyer's [sales cycle](/t/sales-cycle) (quarters, not weeks) means you hire in month 1 and see revenue many months later. A product where a customer can self-serve value in a day is usually cheaper served [product-led](/t/product-led-growth) — the motions have to match the buying behavior, not ambition.

The classic mistakes:

- **Sales before product.** A team selling promises the product can't keep; churn confirms what the demo masked. Founder-led sales ([founder-led sales](/t/founder-led-sales)) is the test that precedes the hire; the [first sales hire](/t/first-sales-hire) should find a repeatable pitch in a known [ICP](/t/ideal-customer-profile), not invent demand.
- **No pipeline source.** Hiring sellers with no inbound or outbound [pipeline](/t/sales-pipeline) behind them just pays salaries to wait.
- **Replacing the founder too early or too late.** Too early: the institutional learning dies. Too late: the founder becomes the bottleneck that also still does the demos.

The hybrid reality: most [SEA B2B](/t/sea-country-differences) and relationship-heavy markets blend — sales opens the door, product-led usage lands the expansion ([expansion revenue](/t/expansion-revenue)). Judge the motion by where each deal's value actually got unlocked.

When not to choose it: low ACV (math kills it), unproven retention (sellers accelerate churn of the wrong customers), or founder bandwidth that is genuinely the cheaper channel — at first.
