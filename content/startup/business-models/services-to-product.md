**Services-to-product** starts as an agency or consultancy, notices the same work repeating across clients, and productizes it. The services phase funds learning: real budgets reveal which problems recur, what they are worth, and what a product must do — intelligence no amount of interviewing buys.

```text
agency:  client 1 pays for custom X → client 2 wants similar X → extract the pattern → product
```

It works because demand is pre-validated by invoices. The transition's difficulty is focus: services revenue rewards saying yes to everything, while products demand saying no to almost everything. Companies die in between — too productized for services margins, too custom for product scale.

The classic mistakes:

- Never making the jump. Comfortable services revenue removes the urgency to productize. Set the transition trigger (repeat count, revenue mix) in advance or the agency wins by default.
- One client's product. Building exactly what the biggest client demands produces custom software with a product label. Productize the pattern across several clients, not the loudest one's spec.
- Services economics wearing product pricing. Custom work at product prices loses money per deal. Price the transition honestly: product margins require product standardization.
- Team mismatch. Service stars (responsive, flexible, client-pleasing) and product builders (opinionated, saying no, playing long games) are different temperaments. Staff the transition deliberately.

**The bridge:** ring-fence product time and people away from client work, sell the emerging product to the next client (not custom work), and let services revenue fund the crossing — then burn the boats on a date, not a feeling.
