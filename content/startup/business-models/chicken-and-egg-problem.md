The **chicken-and-egg problem** is the marketplace paradox: buyers won't come without sellers, sellers won't come without buyers. Every marketplace starts at zero on both sides, and the strategies for escaping zero are the actual business — the platform idea itself is table stakes.

```text
escape routes:  seed one side yourself (founders list first properties, drive first rides)
                constrain to density (one campus, one neighborhood — depth before breadth)
                single-player value first (useful even alone, network second)
                borrow supply (aggregate existing listings with permission-ish creativity)
```

Sequence matters more than balance: pick which side leads (usually supply — sellers tolerate thin demand better than buyers tolerate empty shelves), concentrate them past critical density in one pocket, then let the other side's arrival fund expansion to the next pocket.

The classic mistakes:

- Launching everywhere at once. National launch with ten sellers per city satisfies nobody. One cluster humming beats continental presence every time.
- Subsidizing both sides indefinitely. Paid supply plus paid demand looks like liquidity until the money pauses, then both sides evaporate simultaneously. Subsidies must buy a path to organic density with dated milestones.
- Fake liquidity. Scraped listings, phantom users, inflated counts — discovered quickly, trust destroyed permanently. Seed honestly (founder-supplied, disclosed pilots) or not at all.
- Ignoring single-player value. If the product is useless with few users, every early user churns before density arrives. Build standalone value first so early cohorts stay while the network forms.
- Declaring victory at signing. LOIs and waitlists from both sides feel like escape velocity; only completed transactions count. Measure matches, not interest.

**The test:** transactions happening without subsidy in one dense pocket, repeating weekly. That pocket is the template — replicate it deliberately, pocket by pocket, until the map fills in.
