---
title: "Counter-Positioning"
summary: "A business model that incumbents can't copy without hurting their own business."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Counter-positioning is choosing a business model the incumbent cannot copy without damaging its own revenue, structure or brand — so when they match your offer, they hurt themselves. It is strategy's favorite asymmetry: the big player's *strength* (an established monetization) becomes the reason they cannot follow you.

The classic shapes: challenger gives away what incumbents charge for. A free-to-basic product forces a paid incumbent to either launch a free tier — cannibalizing current revenue — or watch your users grow unbothered. Freemium against per-seat pricing, direct sales against a channel-dependent incumbent (they cannot cut resellers' margins without a revolt — [channel partners](/t/channel-partners) become your moat's wall), subscription bundles against transaction-fee models. The move only counts if copying it is *expensive for them specifically*, not merely unlikely.

```text
positioning:   same game, better moves       (easier to copy)
counter-       different game, copying the    (the trap:
positioning:   move costs them revenue        they must
                                                choose
                                                between
                                                profit
                                                today and
                                                you tomorrow)
```

The founder mistakes: calling any differentiation counter-positioning (a cheaper feature is not a structural conflict for the incumbent); and building the counter-position on a *regulatory or platform* favor that can vanish — model-based disadvantage is durable, permissions-based advantage is not ([platform risk](/t/platform-risk) applies on both ends).

The discipline is the incumbent's P&L: map their revenue lines; the move that attacks the biggest one they defend hardest, or exploits the line *they* must cannibalize, is the one with teeth. If copying you costs them rounding error, it is not a counter-position, just an offer.

Where it is weakest: markets where incumbents face no such conflict — early greenfield categories, or fragmented industries where nobody has a margin to protect. There, [first-mover advantages](/t/first-mover-advantage) and plain execution decide.

The payoff if real: they *cannot* answer you on price or structure, so they attack on brand, distribution and patience — which is exactly when [seven-powers](/t/seven-powers) analysis matters. Counter-positioning buys time; only defensible positions compound it.
