**Platform risk** is dependence on someone else's platform — app stores, social graphs, marketplaces, cloud APIs — that can change rules, pricing or access overnight. Platforms giveth distribution and taketh margin: every business built on one rents its foundation from a landlord with other priorities.

```text
benefits:  instant distribution, trust, payments, users where they are
risks:     30% cuts, API shutdowns, algorithm shifts, policy bans, clone-and-compete
```

Manage it, rarely avoid it — early distribution is worth the risk when the alternative is obscurity. The discipline is optionality proportional to dependence: direct customer relationships (emails, not just followers), multi-platform presence, key features reproducible elsewhere, and revenue mix that survives any single cutoff.

The classic mistakes:

- Single-platform existence. 100% of users, revenue or acquisition through one gatekeeper. Diversify before forced to — the cutoff always comes as a surprise.
- Ignoring ToS evolution. Platforms tighten steadily (API pricing shocks are the modern genre). Read terms as living documents and model fee/rule changes as scenarios.
- Building the platform's roadmap for them. Features that compete with the host getSherlocked — adopted natively, leaving you stranded. Avoid head-on overlap with host incentives; differentiate where they cannot follow.
- No direct relationship. Followers you cannot contact, customers you cannot email. Harvest owned channels (email lists, communities) from day one, whatever the platform.
- App-store single-threading. Review rejections, policy sweeps and commission changes hit mobile-first startups hardest. Web fallbacks and direct distribution hedge the gatekeeper ([app releases](/t/app-store-releases)).

**The question:** "what breaks if they change the rules tomorrow, and how long to recover?" If the answer is existential with no plan B, the platform owns your company — act accordingly before it acts first.
