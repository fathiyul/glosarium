A startup acquisition is one company buying another — for its product, customers, team, technology or market position. For the seller it is the most common real-world exit: far more companies are acquired than list publicly. For the buyer it is buying time: a capability or a foothold cheaper or faster than building, if the price and integration are honest.

The deal anatomy, in order: strategic rationale (product, market, talent, defensive), valuation shaped by that rationale, an LOI ([letter of intent](/t/letter-of-intent)), [due diligence](/t/investor-due-diligence) that tests every claim, then structure — cash at closing, earn-outs tied to future performance, retention packages for key people — and finally integration, the phase most deals lose value in.

```text
what buyers actually pay for:
  revenue/customers .... market share, cross-sell potential
  technology ........... speed-to-capability, IP, data
  team ................. the builders, not the headcount
  defensive ............ removing a rival, blocking a category
what diligence checks: numbers, contracts, IP, taxes, people
```

As a founder selling: the negotiation starts long before the offer — clean [cap table](/t/cap-table), transferable contracts, [audited](/t/financial-audit) numbers and no founder-dependency raise your price more than any pitch deck. Earn-outs are sold as upside but concentrate risk on your team's future under someone else's management; know your walk-away and your [runway](/t/runway) leverage — a buyer sensing a forced sale prices it. As a founder buying: acquire for what you cannot build in the timeframe that matters, budget integration like a product (it is a product), and assume some senior people leave.

The classic mistakes: buying to defend rather than to build (most defensive acquisitions destroy the acquired team's value); integration by spreadsheet (culture and retention decide the outcome within months); and the seller's version — over-reading interest from a single acquirer whose [counter-positioning](/t/counter-positioning) depends on you staying weak.

When it is right: the fit is specific and time-bound; when it is vanity: "we should be acquired someday" as a plan instead of a position built to be worth it. See [acqui-hire](/t/acqui-hire) for the team-focused variant and [exit strategy](/t/exit-strategy) for the broader frame.
