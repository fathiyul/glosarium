---
title: "Acqui-hire"
summary: "An acquisition mainly to hire the team, with the product often shut down."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: scale }
---

An acqui-hire is an acquisition whose main value is the team, not the revenue — the company's customers, product and brand are wound down or absorbed while the people join, usually with retention money attached. It sits between hiring and acquiring: you buy a working group that already ships together, at a price roughly of key-person retention plus IP transfer, often below what the team would cost to hire piecemeal over years.

The typical shape: an offer per retained person plus modest IP/asset consideration, founder and engineer retention vesting over 12-24 months, product sunset or migration with a customer wind-down period. Clean because nobody pretends the revenue line is the point; the diligence is about people and code, not [unit economics](/t/unit-economics).

Why it happens: the target failed at distribution or timing but has a rare team (deep infra, ML, a specific domain); the acquirer faces a capability gap faster to buy than build; and venture-funded teams that cannot raise again often have no better path than dissolution or acqui-hire — the founders' graceful option versus shutting and scattering.

The pitfalls:

- For the *buyer*: acqui-hires integrate badly. A team acquired intact often loses autonomy, and the talent retention the deal was for leaks within a year if the org around them changes their work. People join for mission or money; when the first changes, the second alone is expensive.
- For the *founder*: it can read publicly as failure while being, practically, the best available outcome — communicate honestly with the team; retention terms decide whose outcome it really is. Negotiate for the people you led, not just yourself.
- For *employees*: your equity story inverts — you are the asset and the product shuts. Read retention contracts carefully: cliffs, termination clauses and what happens after the retention window matter more than the headline.

Regulators in some jurisdictions scrutinize acqui-hires as disguised competitor removal — in concentrated tech markets a pattern of absorbing small threats has drawn antitrust attention; check local rules if the strategic goal is real (that would be an [acquisition](/t/startup-acquisition), and be honest in the paper trail either way).

When it is the right tool: specific capability, immediate timeline, team already proven together. When it is not: general headcount (just hire), or as your stated plan from day one — building a team explicitly to sell to acquirers quietly is building on someone else's priorities; the market for teams is thin, cyclical and indifferent to your terms.
