---
title: "Raising Prices"
summary: "Increasing prices for new and existing customers without losing trust."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Raising prices is the fastest profit lever most companies never pull deliberately. A 10% increase with no added cost is 10% straight to contribution margin — but it also spends customer trust, so the craft is sequencing who pays it and when.

The low-risk sequence is: **new customers first.** Every new cohort at the new price compounds growth without touching anyone's renewal. Then existing customers at renewal, ideally paired with what changed — more value shipped, inflation, scope creep on their usage. Grandfathering old plans for a transition period ("your rate held 12 more months") converts a shock into a scheduling problem. The aggressive end — raising prices mid-contract — is almost always wrong unless volume clearly changed.

```text
$100 → $110 for new logos only: 0 churn impact, full margin on growth
$100 → $110 at renewal, with 6-month grace: ~all the profit, most of the trust
mid-term increases on annual contracts: churn spikes, goodwill spent, rarely worth it
```

What makes it work: a value story the customer can repeat to their boss ("they shipped X, Y, Z since last year"), honest communication before invoices not after, and data on which segments absorb it. Elasticity differs wildly: [churn](/t/churn-rate) on a price-sensitive SMB tier may spike while enterprise barely notices. Segment the increase; don't apply one hammer.

What breaks: doing it silently, at scale, in the same quarter as a service regression — the anger is about trust, not money. Or pricing on cost alone; [value-based pricing](/t/value-based-pricing) means increases are defensible when outcomes grew ([customer success](/t/customer-success) health data is your evidence).

When not to raise: pre-PMF (stability and learning beat margin), during a competitive land-grab where price is the wedge ([counter-positioning](/t/counter-positioning) often means *staying* cheap deliberately), or when your [NRR](/t/net-revenue-retention) is already under 100% — fix value before taxing it. And annual small indexed increases, done from day one, are the version of this that never requires the scary meeting.
