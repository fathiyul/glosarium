---
title: "Enterprise Pricing"
summary: "Custom, \"contact sales\" pricing for large customers with large needs."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Enterprise pricing is negotiated, custom pricing for large customers — quotes built per deal rather than a self-serve checkout. It becomes necessary when buyers need procurement sign-off, security review, legal terms and volume commitments, and when value scales with their usage in ways a flat tier cannot capture.

The structure usually layers: a platform fee for core value, [usage-based](/t/usage-based-pricing) or seat-based components that scale, and negotiated discounts for term length or volume. The discipline is keeping the **list price honest** — if custom quotes are what everyone pays, list price is theater and every future discount is baked in from day one.

```text
good enterprise deal: tier floor + volume price + pilot-to-production ramp
bad one:              "same as mid-market but bigger" — negotiated ad hoc,
                      unrepeatable, margin invisible until renewal
```

What founders get wrong:

- Selling the enterprise *story* before the enterprise *motion* exists. Custom pricing without [sales capacity](/t/enterprise-sales), security documentation, support SLAs and delivery muscle converts pilots into churn at renewal.
- Bespoke product drift: each logo requests unique features; pricing them at zero turns your roadmap into a services arm ([seven-powers](/t/seven-powers) process power dies this way).
- Ignoring procurement reality. Large buyers benchmark discounts, want pilots, need multi-year flexibility clauses — know the dance before you sign terms that make renewal a knife fight.

The value math is where you earn the price: anchor to what the deal saves or generates for that buyer ([value-based pricing](/t/value-based-pricing)), not your cost-plus wishes. Expect long [sales cycles](/t/sales-cycle) — quarters, not weeks — and price the CAC accordingly ([unit economics](/t/unit-economics)).

When not to: below roughly a five-figure annual contract value, the selling overhead eats the margin; product-led motion with published [pricing tiers](/t/pricing-tiers) wins there. And one unrepresentative enterprise logo at a desperate price is a strategic error dressed as revenue — it skews your product, your metrics and your pricing memory for years.
