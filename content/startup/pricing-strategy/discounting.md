---
title: "Discounting"
summary: "When discounts help close deals and when they train customers to wait."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

A discount is price given away to win a deal. Used deliberately it closes gaps a customer genuinely has — budget cycles, first-purchase risk, a competitor's incumbent pricing. Used habitually it is a tax on your own future: every discount retrains what buyers expect and what your [sales cycle](/t/sales-cycle) costs to close.

The healthy uses share a shape: the discount buys something you value, not just a signature. Early designs get a case study and a logo. Annual prepay gets a cash-flow win you can actually spend ([cash-flow](/t/cash-flow) and [burn](/t/burn-multiple) both improve). Multi-year gets forecast certainty. A founder relationship gets your first enterprise reference. Each of those trades margin for an asset; a discount that buys nothing is just surrender.

The classic failure modes:

- **Discounting against objections.** Price was never the real blocker; the prospect learned that pushing yields price. The next deal waits three rounds before engaging seriously.
- **Renewal cliffs.** Year one at 50% off rebuilds at 100% next renewal — a churn event you scheduled yourself. Either price the contract honestly or set the ramp explicitly as part of the pilot.
- **Channel rot.** Salespeople route around any approval ladder if founders override it once. One discount matrix, owned by someone, enforced even against your own team.

```text
every discount trades margin for something:
good: term length, prepayment, case study, reference rights, scope certainty
bad:  "they asked", "big logo", "quarter-end panic"
```

Where discounts get systematically bad is when they are the model: reseller and partnership pricing compresses your realized price further every layer ([channel partners](/t/channel-partners)); the published price must survive that. And the permanent fix to habitual discounting is usually [pricing tiers](/t/pricing-tiers) that make the cheap option real (self-serve, lighter support) rather than an invoice edit.

The one time not to worry: launch phase, first ten customers — buy designs aggressively; what you are learning is worth more than the margin. Just record what each discount bought, because renewal day is when the bill arrives.
