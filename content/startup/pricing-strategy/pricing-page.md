The **pricing page** is where evaluation converts or dies: buyers arrive with intent and leave confused, reassured, or committed within a minute. Every element serves one question — "which plan is for me, and what happens when I click?" — and every ambiguity taxes conversion directly.

```text
anatomy:  middle tier highlighted → 3 tiers max → feature rows that differentiate
          → annual/monthly toggle → FAQ killing top objections → trial/demo CTA
```

Write it for skimmers deciding, not readers studying: plan names that signal fit ("Team" beats "Pro Plus"), 5–7 differentiating rows (not 40 checkmarks), prices with billing context, and the trial/talk action one click away. Test it like a landing page, because that is what it is.

The classic mistakes:

- Feature-matrix paralysis. Dozens of rows differing in trivia force buyers to audit instead of choose. Show differences that decide; hide parity in footnotes.
- Hiding prices ("contact us" for everything). Enterprise custom quotes are fine; hiding SMB prices signals fear or chaos. Publish what you can; explain what you cannot.
- No annual option visible. Monthly-only defaults leave cash and retention on the table. Present [annual vs monthly](/t/annual-vs-monthly-billing) as a real choice with real math.
- Trial confusion. "Free trial", "free plan", "demo", "freemium" used interchangeably on one page. Name exactly one no-commitment path ([free trial](/t/free-trial)) and route everything to it.
- Stale page, live product. Plans changed, page fossilized — support tickets and angry cancellations follow. Version the page with releases, and audit quarterly against actual plans.

**Measure:** plan-page-to-trial rate, tier mix vs targets, and support questions about pricing (each one is page copy failing). The pricing page is a conversion asset with revenue attached to every unclear row.
