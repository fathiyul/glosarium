---
title: "Burn Multiple"
summary: "Cash burned for each dollar of new recurring revenue, a measure of growth efficiency."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Burn multiple is how much cash you burn to buy each unit of growth: net monthly [burn](/t/burn-rate) divided by net new ARR (or net new revenue) for the month. Burn $250K, added $125K ARR → 2.0x. Lower means cheaper growth; it is the efficiency lens that [runway](/t/runway) arithmetic alone misses — two companies with identical runway can be buying very different amounts of future.

Common investor shorthand treats under ~1.5x as efficient, ~2-3x as aggressive-but-defensible in a land-grab, and worse as a warning — bands, not laws; multiples depend on how much of the spend is one-time (launch costs, a big hire's ramp) versus steady-state, and on whether growth is decelerating or accelerating in the denominator.

The traps are all in the numerator and denominator hygiene:

- Denominator games: annualizing a single big deal inflates net new ARR; use [NRR](/t/net-revenue-retention)-consistent cohort math.
- Billings confusion: prepaid annual contracts burn cash once and "add" ARR twelve times a year — billings-heavy companies can show fake-cheap growth in a month of renewals.
- Ignoring quality: a 1.5x multiple buying revenue that churns next quarter is really infinityx.

```text
net burn / net new ARR
$250K / $125K = 2.0x  → each $1 of new ARR costs $2 of burn
```

When not to use it: pre-revenue (no denominator), or during deliberate investment phases — building a platform team or opening a new country shows up as terrible multiples while they pay off later. It is a steering wheel, not a scoreboard: pair it with [rule of 40](/t/rule-of-40) at scale, where the question becomes growth plus profitability together, not burn efficiency alone.
