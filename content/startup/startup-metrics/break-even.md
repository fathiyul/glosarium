**Break-even** is revenue covering all costs — the business funding itself, fundraising optional instead of mandatory. It changes everything downstream: negotiating from strength, choosing growth pace freely, surviving market turns without permission. Profitability is independence; everything before it is borrowed time (well spent or not).

```text
break-even when:  gross profit ≥ operating expenses (sustainably, not one lucky month)
paths:            grow revenue into costs · cut costs to revenue · usually both, sequenced
```

One profitable month is not break-even — seasonality, one-offs and delayed bills fake it regularly. Break-even means structural: repeatable revenue covering fully-loaded costs across quarters, with the trajectory holding.

The classic mistakes:

- Declaring victory early. A single good month (annual prepayments banked, hiring paused) misread as transformation. Demand multi-quarter structural evidence before acting on it.
- Cutting to break-even destructively. Slashing growth muscle (sales, R&D) to hit zero-burn quarters trades the future for a headline. Sustainable break-even grows into it; destructive break-even shrinks to it.
- Ignoring cash vs profit. Break-even on accrual with receivables ballooning still kills via cash. Profitability and cash health arrive together or not at all ([cash flow](/t/cash-flow)).
- Profitability as identity. "Profitable and proud" companies sometimes underinvest through winnable windows. Break-even is optionality — exercise it (reinvest aggressively where returns exceed costs) rather than worshipping it.
- Moving the definition. Excluding inconvenient costs ("adjusted" everything) to claim a milestone nobody believes. Standard accounting, fully loaded, or the claim poisons every later number.

**Use break-even as:** freedom to choose pace (raise to accelerate or grow on profits), leverage in every negotiation, and the fallback that makes bold bets survivable.
