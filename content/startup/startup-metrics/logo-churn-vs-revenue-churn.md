---
title: "Logo Churn vs Revenue Churn"
summary: "Losing customers versus losing revenue, and why they can tell different stories."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Logo churn counts customers lost; revenue churn counts money lost. They are different dashboards because they fail differently: a company can lose a quarter of its logos while barely denting revenue (small accounts leaving), or keep nearly every logo while revenue bleeds (one enterprise downgrades, or expansion stalling). Which one you optimize has to follow from which pain you actually have.

```text
start: 400 logos / $200K ARR
lost:  30 logos / $25K ARR
logo churn 7.5%  ·  revenue churn 12.5%
→ the big accounts are the unhealthy ones
```

That mismatch is the interesting part. Logo churn running hot on small accounts usually means onboarding, self-serve UX or price-value gap ([customer success](/t/customer-success) programs fix it one cohort at a time). Revenue churn concentrated in a few accounts is [concentration risk](/t/net-revenue-retention), not a funnel problem — losing 2 customers at $50K hurts more than losing 60 at $1K, and the mitigation is diversification and account-level health monitoring, not "more retention."

The classic reporting mistakes: showing only logo churn while big accounts quietly shrink (revenue says), or reporting only revenue churn to hide that the customer base is hollowing out (logos say). Also watch the interaction: heavy logo churn at low price point is what inflates blended CAC payback while the revenue number looks fine — read all three together ([CAC payback](/t/cac-payback-period)).

When not to chase logo churn: deliberate strategy shifts. If you are moving upmarket, exiting SMB on purpose, logo churn spikes while [NRR](/t/net-revenue-retention) and margins improve — that is the plan working, not a fire. Judge churn against the segmentation you chose ([customer segments](/t/customer-segments)), and segment before you panic.
