---
title: "Bookings, Billings and Revenue"
summary: "Three different numbers for \"money from customers\", and why mixing them up misleads."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: growth }
---

Bookings, billings and revenue are three different counts of "money from customers," and mixing them up is how companies convince themselves they are growing faster than their bank account agrees.

**Bookings** are signed commitments: a deal worth $12,000/year closes in June → 12K bookings in June. They are a sales-performance and forecast number, no accounting standard governs them, and companies define them inconsistently (some count only countersigned contracts, some count verbal commitments, some annualize monthly deals aggressively). **Billings** are invoices issued: the $12K prepaid annually is billed in June → 12K billings. Billings is the closest proxy to cash coming in. **Revenue** is what accounting recognizes as earned: under the accrual rules ([revenue recognition](/t/revenue-recognition)), the June prepayment is $1K/month — only 1K of June revenue, 6K by December.

```text
one $12K/yr deal signed + prepaid in June:
  June bookings: 12K   June billings: 12K   June revenue: 1K
```

The classic mistakes: presenting bookings as ARR growth in a pitch deck while revenue lags months behind (investors find out from the [data room](/t/data-room)); calling billings "revenue" in board slides; and the reverse trap — judging a sales-heavy prepay model purely on recognized revenue so growth looks dead the quarter after a big push. Different audiences need different numbers, so label every number on every slide with which one it is.

When the distinctions matter most: [cash-flow](/t/cash-flow) planning (billings and collections), sales compensation (bookings), accounting and fundraising claims (revenue). When they matter least: steady-state monthly-subscription businesses where all three converge within noise. The metric that bridges them is what you actually care about — growing cash-adjusted profit — so keep the three numbers and the reconciliation between them simple enough to explain in one slide.
