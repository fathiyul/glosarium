A **product launch** is the coordinated public release: the product is ready enough, the story is sharp, and every channel fires at once to concentrate attention into signups, feedback and momentum. Launches do not make products succeed — but a good launch compresses months of discovery into days.

```text
before:  waitlist built, onboarding ready, analytics in, support staffed, story written
day:     announce everywhere at once (communities, press, customers, network)
after:   respond to everything, fix fast, convert attention into retained users
```

The real goals in order: feedback (what breaks, what confuses), users (especially the right early adopters), and only then attention. A launch that trends but teaches nothing and retains nobody was theater.

The classic mistakes:

- Launching to nobody. No waitlist, no community, no press list — release day arrives silently. Build the audience *before* the launch, not with it.
- Launching too late. Polishing for six months while competitors ship and learn. Launch when the core value works for forgiving users, then iterate in public.
- One shot mentality. Treating launch as the single moment that decides everything. Most successful products launched quietly, repeatedly — every feature, integration and milestone is a smaller launch.
- Unstaffed launch day. Traffic spikes, bugs surface, questions flood in — with nobody watching. All hands on deck, analytics live, rollback ready.
- No follow-through. Attention decays in days. Capture emails, onboard personally, ship the top requests visibly within weeks — convert the spike into a base (see [launch channels](/t/launch-channels)).

**After launch:** measure what the spike taught, keep what worked as always-on channels, and return to the loop — launch is an event inside continuous iteration, not its conclusion.
