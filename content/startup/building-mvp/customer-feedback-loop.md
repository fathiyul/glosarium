A **customer feedback loop** is the routine that turns what users say into what you build: collect from every channel, sort by frequency and severity, decide visibly, and close the loop by telling users what changed. Without the routine, feedback piles up in chat logs and support tickets until it is simultaneously overwhelming and useless.

```text
collect (support, chat, calls, reviews) → sort (bugs vs requests vs confusion)
→ decide (now / later / never, with reasons) → tell users → repeat weekly
```

The sorting matters more than the collecting. Bugs by severity, requests by frequency × strategic fit, confusion as UX signal. And every "never" needs telling too — users who hear why a request was declined trust the next decision more, not less.

The classic mistakes:

- Collecting without deciding. A thousand-item backlog that grows and is never triaged. Cap the inbox: weekly triage, decisions recorded, list pruned ruthlessly.
- Building by loudest voice. One enterprise prospect's demand outweighing a hundred small users' need — unless that prospect's contract funds the quarter. Weight by evidence, not volume.
- No closing of the loop. Shipping requested features silently teaches users that feedback vanishes. Announce what changed because of whom; it multiplies future feedback quality.
- Confusing feedback with strategy. Users report solutions ("add a button"); your job is the underlying job ([jobs to be done](/t/jobs-to-be-done)). Mine requests for problems, design the solutions yourself.
- Support as the only channel. Support tickets skew toward the broken and the angry. Balance with interviews, usage data and [beta cohorts](/t/beta-program) for the full picture.

**Cadence:** weekly triage, monthly themes review, visible changelog always. The loop's output is not just features — it is users who feel heard, which is retention fuel.
