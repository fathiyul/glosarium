**Design partners** are a handful of early customers who co-build the product with you: they give time, feedback and real use cases; you give influence over the roadmap, white-glove treatment and early (often discounted) access. Three to five engaged partners teach more than a hundred casual signups.

```text
partner gives:  weekly feedback, real data, intros, patience with bugs
you give:      roadmap influence, hand-holding, favorable pricing, responsiveness
```

Formalize lightly: what each side commits (time, feedback cadence, pilot scope), for how long, and what happens after. A short written understanding prevents the slow drift into unpaid consulting.

The classic mistakes:

- Too many partners. Ten partners means ten roadmaps and no product. Three to five, chosen for representativeness of the target segment — not whoever said yes first.
- Building custom features per partner. Each partner's special request feels existential when they are your only users. Build the pattern across partners; say no with the roadmap as shield.
- Free forever. Perpetual free access removes the pricing signal you most need. Time-box the special terms and convert to paid ([paid pilot](/t/paid-pilot)) on schedule.
- Partners unlike future customers. Friendly startups tolerate chaos that enterprises never will (or vice versa). If partners don't resemble the target market, their feedback misleads — recruit for representativeness.

**Graduate them:** into paying customers with contracts, reference logos and introductions. A design partner who won't pay or refer was market research, not validation — thank them and move on.
