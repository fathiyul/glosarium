**Anti-dilution protection** reprices earlier investors' shares if a later round prices lower (a [down round](/t/down-round)): broad-based weighted average (the market standard — moderate adjustment) or full ratchet (reprice all the way down — brutal to founders). It shifts down-round pain from investors to common holders — i.e., founders and employees.

```text
broad-based weighted average:  modest extra shares to earlier investors (standard, livable)
full ratchet:                  earlier price reset fully to new low (founder wipeout territory)
```

Accept broad-based weighted average as market; resist full ratchet almost always — it converts a bad round into a cap-table catastrophe that can demotivate the team into further decline. And understand pay-to-play variants (lose protection unless you reinvest), which punish non-participating investors instead.

The classic mistakes:

- Signing full ratchet unknowingly. Buried in definitions, discovered at the down round when it multiplies damage. Read every financing document's anti-dilution section with counsel, every time.
- Ignoring it until triggered. Protectionanders sleep for years, then detonate in one round. Model down-round scenarios (with ratchet math) at every fundraise, not during one.
- Triggering it carelessly. Small bridge notes or option repricings at lower valuations can trip broad provisions accidentally. Structure interim financings with ratchet awareness.
- Forgetting employee impact. Ratchet-driven founder/common dilution hits employee option value directly — retention damage on top of financial damage. Communicate and refresh deliberately post-round.
- Treating all down rounds alike. A small inside-led adjustment differs totally from a cram-down recapitalization. The label matters less than the specific mechanics and who bears what.

**Negotiate once, model always:** broad-based weighted average standard, full ratchet refused, scenarios modeled per round. Down rounds are survivable; surprise mechanics are not.
