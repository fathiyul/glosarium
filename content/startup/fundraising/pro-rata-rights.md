**Pro rata rights** let investors buy enough of each future round to maintain their ownership percentage — countering [dilution](/t/dilution) round after round. Standard for major investors, valuable signal when exercised (insiders doubling down), ominous when pointedly not ([signaling risk](/t/signaling-risk)).

```text
owns 10% → next round: right to buy 10% of it (maintains 10%, pays full new price)
no discount for loyalty — the right is access and allocation, not price
```

Manage the cap table with pro-rata in mind: rounds need room for insider follow-ons plus new money, or allocation fights erupt at close. And track who exercises — participation patterns across rounds write the story future investors read first.

The classic mistakes:

- Promising pro rata to everyone. Small angels with follow-on rights complicate every future round's allocation math. Reserve pro rata for major investors; keep small checks clean and simple.
- Super pro rata (above ownership share). Rights to *grow* stakes sound flattering until they crowd out new money the round needs. Cap follow-on rights at maintaining share, with growth by mutual agreement.
- Ignoring non-exercise signals. A lead passing pro rata two rounds running says more than any board comment. Ask directly, early — the answer shapes strategy while options remain.
- Allocation warfare at close. New lead wants full allocation, insiders claim pro rata, math doesn't fit — negotiated under time pressure with bruised feelings. Model allocations (new + pro rata + pool) before opening the round.
- Forgetting pay-to-play linkages. Some structures tie continued rights (and anti-dilution) to participation — skip a round, lose protections. Understand the linkage before sitting any round out.

**Treat pro rata as relationship infrastructure:** grant to partners worth keeping, track exercise as signal, and model every round's allocation with insiders included from the first spreadsheet.
