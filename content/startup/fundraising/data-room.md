A **data room** is the organized document set shared under NDA with investors (fundraising) or acquirers (M&A): corporate records, cap table, financials, contracts, IP docs, team details, metrics. diligence stalls without one and flies with one — preparation quality reads as company quality.

```text
structure:  corporate (formation, board minutes) · capitalization (table, grants, SAFEs)
            financials (P&L, model, bank statements) · legal (contracts, IP, litigation: none hoped)
            commercial (metrics, pipeline, references) · team (org, comp, key hires)
```

Build it before fundraising starts and maintain it monthly: every new contract, grant and metric lands in the room the week it happens. A room assembled in panic under term-sheet deadlines is where discrepancies breed.

The classic mistakes:

- Building it at term sheet. Weeks of scrambling while momentum bleeds. The room predates the process — start it at incorporation and grow it steadily.
- Raw dumps instead of curation. Five hundred unsorted files answer nothing. Organize by diligence area with an index; reviewers should find anything in a minute.
- Stale numbers. Last quarter's metrics in a live process invite exactly the questions you least want. Refresh financials and metrics monthly during active raises.
- Over-sharing early. Full contracts and customer names to tire-kickers leak competitively. Tier access: teaser metrics widely, sensitive documents post-term-sheet under NDA.
- Hiding the bad drawer. Missing board minutes, unsigned grants, verbal agreements — discovered gaps detonate trust. Fix or disclose everything before reviewers find it (see [diligence](/t/investor-due-diligence)).

**Maintain continuously:** new documents filed weekly, metrics refreshed monthly, full review quarterly. A ready room turns diligence from an ordeal into a formality — and signals the operational excellence investors are actually buying.
