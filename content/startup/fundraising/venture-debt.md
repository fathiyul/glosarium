**Venture debt** is loans to venture-backed startups — typically taken alongside equity rounds, sized as fractions of the raise, repaid over a few years with interest plus warrants (small equity kickers). It extends [runway](/t/runway) without priced-round dilution: the equity round sets the valuation story, debt funds the months between milestones.

```text
shape:  raise $5M equity → add $1–2M debt → 6–12 extra months for milestones (roughly)
cost:   interest + fees + warrant coverage (single-digit equity %) + covenants
```

Lenders underwrite the *investors* as much as the company (VC backing as credit signal), plus growth and cash position. It suits revenue-generating, venture-backed companies smoothing between rounds — not pre-revenue experiments or turnarounds, where debt covenants become existential threats.

The classic mistakes:

- Debt for experiments. Borrowing to fund unproven bets converts equity risk (lose investors' money) into survival risk (miss payments, trigger covenants). Debt funds proven motions needing time, never discovery.
- Covenant blindness. Minimum cash, material-adverse-change clauses, investor-abandonment triggers — tripped exactly when weakest. Read covenants as scenarios (what happens in the bad quarter?) not boilerplate.
- Warrant math ignored. "Small" warrant coverage compounds across facilities into real dilution, often at painful valuations. Price total cost (interest + fees + warrants) against equity alternatives honestly.
- Stacking facilities. Multiple lenders with intercreditor complexity and cross-defaults turn one stumble into a cascade. One facility, understood deeply, beats three cheap ones.
- Timing it late. Arranging debt during distress means predatory terms or no offers. Secure facilities from strength (post-round, growing) for use in need — credit lines, not rescue loans.

**Use it as:** a runway bridge between priced rounds for companies with revenue and backing — sized conservatively, covenanted carefully, and never as a substitute for the economics working. See [RBF](/t/revenue-based-financing) for the lighter sibling and [bridge rounds](/t/bridge-round) for the equity alternative.
