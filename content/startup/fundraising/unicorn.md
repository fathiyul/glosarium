A **unicorn** is a private company valued at $1B+ — originally a rarity-describing coinage, now a scoreboard with hundreds of names. The label measures one thing (last round's paper valuation) while implying many others (success, durability, wealth) that often are not true: paper valuations reverse in down rounds, common holders may see little, and unicorn status has bankrupted as many strategies as it validated.

```text
unicorn = last preferred price × fully-diluted shares (paper, conditional, reversible)
≠ cash generated ≠ common-stock value ≠ durability (all separate questions)
```

Treat it as others' milestone for you, never as strategy: optimize the business (unit economics, retention, real growth), and let valuations follow. Companies built to *become unicorns* raise at any terms, burn structurally, and discover the label's hollowness in the downturn that tests preferences and ratchets.

The classic mistakes:

- Valuation as identity. Teams internalizing the number as worth — spending, hiring and behaving like the valuation is cash. Paper wealth organizes activity only when converted (exit, secondary); until then it is modeling.
- Dirty unicorn math. Rounds with heavy preferences, ratchets and structure manufacturing $1B+ headlines on fragile economics. Sophisticated observers discount structure; unsophisticated ones get hurt later.
- Chasing the label with terms. Accepting any structure to cross the line (participating preferred, full ratchets, aggressive liquidation stacks) mortgages the entire cap table for a press release.
- Ignoring the common-stock gap. Billion-dollar preferred valuations with deep preference stacks can leave founders and employees near zero in moderate exits. Model *common* outcomes, not headlines ([waterfall](/t/liquidation-preference)).
- Regional unicorn theater. "First unicorn of X" narratives pressuring premature scaling and spending. Build the company; let geographies celebrate on their own schedule.

**Keep the label in perspective:** a milestone others grant, reversible, and strategically inert. Run toward economics and durability; valuations follow deserving companies more reliably than chased ones.
