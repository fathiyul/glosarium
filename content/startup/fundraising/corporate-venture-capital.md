**Corporate venture capital (CVC)** invests off corporate balance sheets for strategic plus financial returns: market intelligence, startup pipelines, partnership options — and, eventually, acquisition paths. Checks can be generous and validation valuable, but corporate incentives (strategy shifts, budget cycles, internal politics) differ fundamentally from fund incentives (returns, period).

```text
CVC pros:  deep pockets · distribution partnerships · industry credibility · acquisition path
CVC cons:  slow decisions · shifting priorities · information risks · follow-on uncertainty
```

Diligence CVCs harder than VCs: fund lifecycle (evergreen or budget-annual?), decision process (who can kill it mid-process?), strategic intent (complement or future competitor?), and track record with founders specifically (references from portfolio CEOs, especially the failed ones).

The classic mistakes:

- Strategic money without strategic value. A corporate logo on the cap table that opens no doors, shares no data, makes no introductions — expensive decoration at the cost of complications. Demand concrete partnership mechanics upfront.
- Information leakage. Sharing roadmap and metrics with a potential competitor's investment arm. Stage disclosure, protect the crown jewels, and understand information rights fully before signing.
- Right-of-first-refusal traps. Acquisition rights, matching rights and exclusivities that block better exits later. These clauses outlive everyone's memory of why they seemed fine — negotiate as if enforced adversarially.
- Depending on one corporate. Single-CVC dependence makes the startup hostage to reorgs and strategy pivots. Diversify investors; never let one corporate control survival.
- Signaling confusion. Top-tier VCs sometimes read CVC-led rounds as "couldn't raise institutional" ([signaling](/t/signaling-risk)). Sequence thoughtfully — institutional lead plus strategic follow-on reads strongest.

**Take CVC as complement, rarely as lead:** partnership value contracted specifically, information protected structurally, and institutional investors anchoring the round. Done right it accelerates; done carelessly it entangles.
