A **bridge round** is interim funding — usually from existing investors via notes or SAFEs — that extends [runway](/t/runway) to a milestone unlocking the next priced round. Legitimate uses: stellar progress needing six more months to prove out, or market timing mismatches. Illegitimate use (most common): postponing the reckoning on a company the market has already judged.

```text
good bridge:  metrics strong, milestone 6 months out, insiders compete to fund it
bad bridge:   flat growth, no insider enthusiasm, bridge funds the hope that hope returns
```

Insider participation is the signal: if existing investors — who know everything — won't bridge, outsiders read it correctly as a verdict. A bridge led by outsiders on clean terms, conversely, can re-rate the story.

The classic mistakes:

- Bridge to nowhere. Raising six months with no credible path to different facts at the end. Define the milestone the bridge buys and the decision if missed — in writing, before wiring.
- Stacking bridges. Bridge after bridge, each smaller and harsher, normalizes decline while burning team years. Two bridges without progress is the data; act on it ([shutting down](/t/shutting-down) is an honorable option).
- Punitive insider terms. Desperation pricing (capped conversions, warrants, control grabs) that demotivates founders exactly when motivation matters most. Fair bridge terms preserve the team to fight another round.
- Hiding the bridge. Announcing priced rounds while quietly bridging confuses everyone and leaks eventually. Treat team and key stakeholders as adults: situation, plan, milestones.
- Skipping the hard conversation. Bridging avoids deciding (pivot? shut down? acqui-hire?) by buying time without a thesis. Time without thesis is the most expensive input there is.

**The test:** name the milestone, the date, and the decision if missed — agreed with the board before money moves. Bridges buy specific evidence, never general hope.
