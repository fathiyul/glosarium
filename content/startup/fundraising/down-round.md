---
title: "Down Round"
summary: "Raising at a lower valuation than the previous round, and its consequences."
levels: { tech-entrepreneur: scale }
---

A down round is a financing priced below the previous round's valuation — new money comes in at a post-money lower than the last one's. It happens when growth stalled, markets repriced, or [runway](/t/runway) ran out with leverage gone; the company still needs cash and the price no longer supports the old number.

The mechanics and damage:

- **Anti-dilution triggers.** Preferred stock protection ([anti-dilution](/t/anti-dilution-protection)) usually kicks in — broad-based weighted-average resets are the common default; full-ratchet (rare and brutal) would reprice old preferred all the way to the new price, gutting founders and employees further.
- **Preference stacking.** Investors may demand the new money get senior [liquidation preference](/t/liquidation-preference) — in a later sale, everyone below gets paid later and less; the [cap table](/t/cap-table) becomes a waterfall with more steps.
- **Human cost.** Option strikes sit above the new mark ([vesting](/t/vesting-schedule) holders underwater), recruiting and retention narratives break, and the round's signal invites "what did they know."

The classic mistake is treating any down round as the alternative to death *as-is*: sometimes it is the right rescue — recapitalize, reset expectations, clean the table so the company survives to grow again. The mistake is a down round that *only* reprices: same burn, same plan, less credibility, worse terms — the pre-down bridge or extension ([bridge round](/t/bridge-round)) at flat valuation, or a restructure negotiated with existing preferred, may be the cleaner move.

What makes one survivable: new money aligned to a fixed plan (cut burn, prove the metric the last round assumed), existing investors participating where possible (their absence is itself a signal), and a founder conversation with the team about the reset — hiding the repricing until retention conversations turn awkward makes it worse.

When to accept it quickly: when [cash](/t/cash-flow) reaches zero first, the alternative is a fire sale ([acqui-hire](/t/acqui-hire)) or closure; a controlled down round beats a desperate one. When not to: if you can cut to a path with existing runway, raising at any mark may be the most expensive option you have.
