The **NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha)** is the business identification number issued through Indonesia's [OSS system](/t/oss-rba): one number that functions as company registration, and — depending on risk class — as the basic license to operate. For low-risk business lines it is effectively the whole license; higher-risk lines need further permits layered on top.

```text
deed + approval → OSS application (KBLI lines determine risk class) → NIB issued
low risk:  NIB suffices to operate · higher risk: NIB + additional permits
```

Get it early: banks, marketplaces, payment partners and enterprise customers ask for it, and operating sizable revenue without one compounds legal risk. Keep the underlying data (address, shareholders, KBLI lines) current — an NIB describing last year's company invites trouble at exactly the moments (fundraise, audit, partnership) when scrutiny peaks.

The classic mistakes:

- Trading without one. Informal operation works until it doesn't — a dispute, a tax notice or a partner's compliance check turns informality into liability overnight.
- Assuming NIB equals all permits. It is the base layer. Sectoral licenses (finance, health, education, logistics) still apply on top; check your [KBLI](/t/kbli) risk class rather than assuming.
- Stale company data. Moved office, changed shareholders, pivoted business lines — unupdated records mismatch every counterparty check. Update with changes, not yearly.
- DIYing complex cases. Straightforward local PTs can self-serve; foreign shareholders, regulated lines or group structures deserve a consultant who knows the current OSS behavior.

**Sequence:** deed → approval → NIB → [NPWP](/t/npwp) tax number → sectoral permits as needed. See [OSS RBA](/t/oss-rba) for the system behind it.
