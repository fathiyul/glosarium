Indonesian labor law distinguishes **PKWT** (fixed-term contracts, for temporary/project work with maximum durations and renewal rules) from **PKWTT** (permanent/indefinite employment). The type determines end-of-contract rights, severance exposure ([pesangon](/t/severance-pay)), and how endings must be handled — choosing wrong (or blurring the line in practice) creates liability that surfaces at termination.

```text
PKWT:  fixed term, specific work, ends by expiry (with statutory completion pay rules)
PKWTT: indefinite, full protections, endings follow dismissal/severance procedures
```

Use PKWT for genuinely temporary needs (projects, seasonal peaks, probation-like evaluation where allowed) and PKWTT for the team you are building. The law restricts PKWT scope precisely because misclassification is the classic abuse — regulators look at substance over labels.

The classic mistakes:

- Permanent roles on rolling PKWTs. Renewing fixed terms indefinitely for core team members invites reclassification plus backdated obligations. Permanent work gets permanent contracts.
- Verbal terms, written surprises. Offer letters promising one thing, contracts stating another, practice doing a third. Align all three before day one ([employment contract](/t/employment-contract)).
- Termination improvisation. Ending either type without following its procedure (notice, severance computation, bipartite settlement attempts) converts routine exits into disputes. Procedure first, always.
- Ignoring regulatory movement. Labor rules (including the omnibus-law lineage reforms) shift rights and thresholds. Confirm current law with counsel at each hiring wave, not once at founding.
- Foreign staff on local templates. Expat employment layers immigration status atop labor type. Coordinate both or risk a contract valid in one regime and void in practice.

**Default:** PKWTT for builders, PKWT only for genuinely temporary work, everything written, procedures followed to the letter. See [letting people go](/t/letting-people-go) for endings done right.
