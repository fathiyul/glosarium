**UMP (Upah Minimum Provinsi)** is the provincial minimum wage set yearly in Indonesia: the legal floor for monthly pay, varying by province (and by city/regency where local minimums apply). Every salary offer, budget and payroll run in the country starts above this line — it is the number everything else adds to.

```text
set yearly per province (sometimes city-level) → floor for full-time monthly pay
use for:  offer floors, budget minima, compliance checks, payroll audits
```

Track the annual decisions for every province you hire in: new rates typically apply from January, announced late the prior year. Payroll systems, offer letters and budgets all need updating in that window — the year's first payroll is the traditional moment to discover last year's numbers.

The classic mistakes:

- One national number assumed. Jakarta and Central Java differ enormously; applying the wrong province's floor underpays illegally or overpays wastefully. Look up each work location separately.
- Allowances mishandled. Which allowances count toward the minimum versus sit on top follows specific rules — getting this wrong underpays even when the headline salary looks compliant. Structure offers with the rules in hand.
- Below-minimum "internships" and probation pay. Trainee and probation arrangements still face floors and rules; discounted pay without legal basis is back-pay plus penalties waiting. Check the category before discounting.
- Annual decision missed. Rates change most years; payroll running on autopilot sails past January with stale numbers. Calendar the annual announcement and update before the first run.
- Forgetting total cost. Minimum wage is the floor of salary, not of employment cost — social security ([BPJS](/t/bpjs)), holiday allowance ([THR](/t/thr)) and taxes stack on top. Budget fully-loaded, never headline alone.

**No rates quoted here** — provinces reset yearly. Look up current figures per work location each December, bake them into offers and budgets, and keep payroll current from January's first run.
