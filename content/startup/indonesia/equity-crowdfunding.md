summary: "Raising from many small investors through licensed platforms."

Securities crowdfunding (SCF) in Indonesia lets a growing business raise modest amounts from many small investors through an OJK-licensed platform, in exchange for shares — a route between family funding and a proper priced round. The rules live under OJK's SCF framework (the former EF-UMKM regime); the practical picture: a licensed platform vets your company, you publish an offer, accredited-ish limits apply for ordinary investors, and the shares come out of a properly structured PT.

The shape, in direction (thresholds change — verify current rules with counsel): issuers must be small/medium businesses within revenue and asset limits; the platform performs due diligence on documents and disclosure; investor holdings per issuer and per year are capped; the company must have its deeds, shareholders and reporting in order — which is itself a filter, since [PT](/t/pt-perseroan-terbatas) formalities (notaris deeds, tax ID [NPWP](/t/npwp), AHU-online filings) decide whether you can issue at all ([OSS/NIB](/t/nib) and KBLI codes matter for registration accuracy too).

Why founders pick it: capital without the venture dilution math, from a market of Indonesians who want to back local businesses; validation in a consumer-facing story ("crowds own part of us" sells); and a bridge when a VC round isn't right — small, priced equity without a full fundraise cycle.

Why founders avoid it: the amounts are small relative to a venture push; investor lists of hundreds of tiny holders complicate every later round (cap-table [hygiene](/t/cap-table) — platforms mitigate by aggregating holdings, but read the structure); disclosure is now semi-public forever; and a foreign-held [Singapore structure](/t/singapore-holding-structure) above the PT may restrict how shares can be issued locally at all.

The realistic use: consumer brands with a community, early-stage PTs with clean paperwork, founders who value non-dilutive-of-control capital over size. Treat the platform's listing process like a mini [due diligence](/t/investor-due-diligence): if your papers are not ready for 500 small shareholders, they are not ready for one institutional investor either — the license just makes that gap visible earlier.
