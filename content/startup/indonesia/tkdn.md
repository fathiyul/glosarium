TKDN — Tingkat Komponen Dalam Negeri, or local content level — is Indonesia's measure of how much of a product was made domestically, calculated from local materials, labor and manufacturing processes and certified under Ministry of Industry rules. It exists to steer procurement — especially government purchasing — toward local production, and it shows up wherever public money buys physical or tech-adjacent goods.

Where startups meet it, in practical order:

```text
government/soe tenders:   TKDN score (with a local-industry
                          bonus) gives bid preference — and for
                          some categories a floor; selling
                          electronics, devices or infrastructure
                          without a certified number means
                          losing on math, not merit
regulated procurement:    telecom equipment and network
                          rollout programs have historically
                          carried local-content conditions
enterprise buyers:        SOE subsidiaries and regulated
                          industries mirror the rule in their
                          sourcing
```

The certified number is a calculation across components, manufacturing and labor — verified through an audit process — and thresholds change with each procurement scheme, so the working rule is: get the certificate from a licensed verification body if you sell into public demand, and check the current percentage floors per tender rather than a remembered figure.

The founder mistake pattern: software and hardware-adjacent startups assuming "it's just code" escapes the rules, then discovering a tender defines the delivered device, box or bundled hardware as the product; and the reverse trap — outsourcing the paperwork to a consultant who manufactures a number the audit cannot defend. There is a real strategy version too: for hardware and industrial startups, hitting the threshold early is a cornered-resource play in [public procurement](/t/regulatory-risk) — many competitors structurally cannot, so the certificate plus the score becomes the moat in that channel.

For most digital-only SaaS founders TKDN is a non-issue — until the day the product ships a device. Know which of those two companies you are running.
