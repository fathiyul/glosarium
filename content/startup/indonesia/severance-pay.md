**Pesangon** is the severance Indonesian employers owe when employment ends: statutory formulas combining severance pay, service appreciation and compensation rights, scaled by tenure and termination grounds. Amounts grow with years of service — a five-year employee's exit costs multiples of a first-year one — which makes hiring-for-life and firing-casually asymmetric bets founders must price in advance.

```text
components:  severance (by tenure) + service reward (by tenure) + outstanding rights (leave, etc.)
driver:      tenure × grounds — longer service and employer-initiated ends cost more
```

Budget it as accruing liability, not surprise expense: every payroll should conceptually reserve the growing obligation. And process matters as much as math — bipartite settlement attempts and proper procedure precede lawful endings; skipping steps converts payments into disputes regardless of amounts.

The classic mistakes:

- Pricing hires on salary alone. Fully-loaded cost includes accruing severance exposure. A senior hire is a multi-year severance commitment entered on day one — model it.
- Discovering formulas at termination. Computing obligations mid-conflict, under emotion and time pressure, guarantees errors and mistrust. Know the formulas before hiring, recompute before acting.
- Procedural shortcuts. Paying generously but skipping required settlement steps still produces disputes — process and payment are separate obligations. Follow both.
- Mass-layoff improvisation. Scale triggers additional procedures and scrutiny. Plan workforce reductions with counsel from the first sketch, never as a Friday decision.
- Assuming startup exemption. Early-stage, foreign-backed, or "family-like" — none exempts from statutory duties. Size offers no shield; compliance scales down to two-person companies.

**No amounts quoted here** — formulas and minima move with regulation. Confirm current computations with counsel per case, budget accruals continuously, and handle every exit by the book. See [letting people go](/t/letting-people-go) for the human half.
