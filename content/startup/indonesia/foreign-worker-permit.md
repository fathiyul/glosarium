Foreign worker permits in Indonesia are the approvals a company must hold before legally employing a foreign national (TKA — tenaga kerja asing): an employment plan approved by the Ministry of Manpower (the RPTKA process, historically paired with the work permit IMTA, now flowing through the online [OSS](/t/oss-rba) workflow) and the accompanying stay permits (KITAS) for the person. Foreigners cannot simply be hired; the sponsorship company carries the compliance.

The durable rules of thumb (practice has shifted with recent labor reforms — verify current requirements with counsel):

```text
plan first, hire second: the role, location and duration are
approved before the person arrives
local staffing: rules and ratios around hiring foreigners
generally pair them with local transfer/development obligations
position gates: advisers, managers and specialists for roles
Indonesian workers are not yet filling — not shop-floor work
reporting: periodic reporting, and deregistration when the
role ends
```

The [PT PMA](/t/pt-pma) angle matters: foreign-capital companies face the investment-value thresholds and KBLI-accurate registration that shape whether a given permit route is even available.

The classic founder mistakes: relocating a founder or senior engineer on a business visa and "sorting permits later" — enforcement fines and deportation risk land on the sponsoring entity, and a messy [data room](/t/data-room) in the next raise surfaces it; budgeting permits as a one-time fee rather than recurring time (processing, reporting, and the exit process when someone resigns mid-KITAS); and treating the local-development obligation as paper — it is also the argument for *why* you hire the foreign expert: to build the local team around them.

The honest planning: the permit timeline belongs in every senior-hire plan measured in months; many [SEA regional](/t/regional-expansion-sea) startups staff regional roles from Singapore or KL precisely to reduce the permit load in Jakarta while building local leadership. And [BPJS](/t/bpjs) and payroll rules stack on top for any local hires you *do* make — foreign-worker compliance is one layer of the employment regime, not the whole thing.
