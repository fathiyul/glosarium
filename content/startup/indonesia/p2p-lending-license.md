A P2P lending license in Indonesia is the OJK registration/licensing status that lets a platform legally connect lenders with borrowers — under OJK's rules governing information-technology-based lending services. Operating lending without it is a sweep target: OJK has repeatedly shut and prosecuted illegal platforms, and the legit path is one of the most common licensing routes among Indonesian fintechs.

What the regulated surface covers (direction, not detail — verify current OJK rules):

- **Entity and capital** — a local company, minimum paid-in capital with staged increases, sponsor/shareholder fitness tests.
- **Conduct** — disclosure of total borrowing cost, fair collection practices, limits on aggressive data use, ethical marketing; OJK publishes lists of licensed and illegal platforms.
- **Data and systems** — reporting to OJK, IT governance, increasingly data handling under [UU PDP](/t/uu-pdp).

The economics founders should model before chasing the license: lending is the fintech category where [unit economics](/t/unit-economics) are most unforgiving. Your take is small per loan, risk is front-loaded and invisible for months, and funding cost (bank partners or your own balance sheet) decides everything. The classic story: a platform grows disbursements fast, wins logos, then discovers vintage losses and a funding partner gone cautious at exactly the same time.

Where the license is actually the strategy: licensed platforms can bank the relationships that unlicensed ones cannot — banks prefer funding compliant platforms, institutional capital requires it, and the sweep cycles repeatedly kill the competitors who skipped it ([regulatory risk](/t/regulatory-risk) flipped into a moat).

Where founders miscalculate: treating registration as the finish line. OJK's conduct supervision is continuous — loan book reporting, debt-collection complaints, interest transparency all keep you accountable after the paper arrives. Plan the compliance function as product scope, not paperwork: who owns it, what it costs monthly, and what it can refuse to ship.
