title: "ESOP in Indonesia"
summary: "How Indonesian startups give employees equity, often through an offshore holding company."

---

ESOP in Indonesia is the practical problem every Indonesian startup hits when it promises employees equity: Indonesian company law for a regular PT (perseroan terbatas) does not have a clean native "stock option" mechanic, so equity promises get built through structures rather than granted directly — and the structure decides the tax, the labor-law view and whether the promise survives the flip.

The common routes (confirm current law and tax treatment with Indonesian counsel — rules and practice evolve):

```text
1. offshore parent grants      the Singapore/Delaware holding
   equity                      issues options/RSUs over its shares;
                               employees hold foreign equity — needs
                               foreign-exchange and tax handling
2. virtual / phantom equity    contractual cash bonus pegged to
   plans                       a notional share value — no real
                               shares, simpler, weaker alignment
3. local shares issued         direct PT shares or conditional
   to employees                grants at the PT level — company
                               formalities, minority-shareholder
                               management, exit friction
```

Route 1 is why [singapore-holding-structure](/t/singapore-holding-structure) and the [Delaware flip](/t/delaware-flip) conversation appears in almost every funded Indonesian startup's legal history — and why investors' term sheets care which entity issues the [option pool](/t/option-pool): the [vesting schedule](/t/vesting-schedule) that matters legally sits in the *granting* entity.

The founder mistakes: promising equity in offer letters with no grant vehicle defined ("the option pool exists" on a spreadsheet is not a security); ignoring that under Indonesian labor practice, termination can feel to employees like forfeiting everything — write the cliff and post-termination exercise rules plainly; and assuming phantom plans are a free shortcut: they carry cash liabilities at exit that hit the [P&L](/t/profit-and-loss) when the liquidity event arrives.

Employee-side truth: ask which entity, which currency, and what happens at tax time; local employees selling foreign-held shares face reporting steps that can surprise everyone. Structure the plan so the company's obligation (payroll tax withholding, reporting) is designed-in, not improvised at grant.
