**Letting people go** — firing for fit/performance or layoffs for business reasons — is inevitable and defining: handled legally, quickly and humanely, it protects the team and the departed; handled badly, it creates legal exposure, cultural rot and reputational damage that outlives the quarter.

```text
performance exit:  clear feedback + PIP with support → decision by deadline → respectful same-day exit
layoff:            business rationale → selection criteria → severance + support → team all-hands after
```

Speed with humanity is the formula: decide fast once clear (limbo tortures everyone), execute same-day once decided (no lame-duck weeks), communicate honestly to both the departed (severance, references, dignity) and the team (reasons without trash-talk, plus what changes).

The classic mistakes:

- Waiting quarters past obvious. Everyone knows, workarounds pile up, top performers absorb the load and leave first. Decide on evidence within weeks, not years.
- Legal improvisation. Skipping procedures, severance computations and settlement steps ([pesangon](/t/severance-pay), [contract types](/t/pkwt-pkwtt)) — disputes cost multiples of proper process. Counsel before action, always.
- Layoffs by spreadsheet alone. Cutting 20% alphabetically keeps politics, loses capability. Cut by future needs with performance data, protect culture carriers deliberately.
- Survivor neglect. The remaining team watches hardest: why them, am I next, does leadership know what it's doing? All-hands honesty, visible plan forward, and attention to morale as a first-class workstream.
- Founder avoidance. Delegating all exits so you never deliver hard news erodes respect exactly when leadership matters most. Hard conversations are the job description — do the worst ones personally.

**The standard:** legal clean, humanely fast, generous within means (severance, references, time), and honest with survivors. Exits done right preserve the ability to hire again — the company's future depends on its reputation with the departed.
