**Co-founder conflict** — over direction, effort, equity or control — kills more startups than competition does. The patterns repeat: unequal commitment discovered too late, strategic disagreements with no decision rule, resentment compounding in silence until explosion or slow quitting. Prevention is paperwork plus candor; cure is rare once trust breaks.

```text
prevent:  written roles + decision rules + vesting + regular honest check-ins
early sign: decisions stall, side conversations multiply, commitment visibly diverges
```

The founder relationship needs maintenance like any critical system: scheduled honest conversations (monthly, structured), explicit decision rights per domain, and vesting that makes departures administrative rather than existential.

The classic mistakes:

- Avoiding the conversation. Discomfort now purchases catastrophe later. Raise divergences the week they appear — small conflicts resolve, large ones detonate.
- No decision mechanism. 50/50 deadlocks with no tiebreaker turn every disagreement into a power struggle. Agree domains of final call in advance ([agreement](/t/co-founder-agreement)).
- Confusing commitment dips with betrayal. Motivation fluctuates (health, family, burnout); treating every dip as disloyalty destroys trust that was merely tired. Distinguish phases from trends before acting.
- Lawyer first, talk never. Legal escalation hardens positions instantly. Talk directly and honestly first, mediate second, litigate last — lawyers formalize outcomes, they rarely produce good ones.
- Keeping a checked-out co-founder. Months of dead equity and dragged decisions from conflict avoidance. The kindest and cheapest resolution is usually a fast, fair, vested exit.

**If it breaks:** execute the written terms (vesting, buyback, roles) swiftly and humanely, communicate once to team and investors with a forward story, and protect the company first — friendships may or may not survive, the company must.
