The **CTO role** transforms as the company grows: coder (you ship everything) → tech lead (you review everything) → manager of managers (you hire people who hire) → executive (technology strategy, org design, board-level bets). Each transition abandons skills that made the last stage work — and founders who refuse the current transition become the bottleneck.

```text
1–10:    ships core systems, sets technical bar, hires first engineers
10–50:   stops coding daily; architecture, hiring, process; managers emerge
50+:     strategy, org design, exec peer; leverage through others entirely
```

The shifts hurt because identity lags role: the coder-founder keeps reviewing every diff at thirty engineers (slowing everything), or avoids management forever (abdicating the actual job). Growth demands the uncomfortable trade — less building, more multiplying.

The classic mistakes:

- Coding past the transition. Founder commits blocking releases, architecture opinions overriding owners. Code for joy on side branches; keep hands off the critical path once owners exist.
- Managing nobody (or everybody directly). Fifteen direct reports is abdication disguised as flatness. Build the management layer deliberately — leads with real authority, not title-only seniors.
- Abandoning technical judgment. Swinging from deep-in-the-code to technology-absent executive who cannot evaluate architecture, hires or vendors. Stay technical through reviews, prototypes and hiring bars without owning delivery.
- Hiring your replacement wrong. The first VPE/CTO-successor hire defines engineering culture for years. Hire slowly, check engineering-leadership references brutally, and keep a path back if it fails.
- Identity crisis unmanaged. Grief for the maker days is real and normal. Name it, find joy in multiplication (others shipping what you enabled), or step into a different role honestly ([founder as CEO](/t/founder-as-ceo), chief architect, IC fellow).

**The throughline:** leverage migrates from hands to heads to orgs. Each stage's superpower becomes the next stage's bottleneck — evolve on purpose, ahead of the pain, not behind it.
