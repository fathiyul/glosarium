**Board meetings** (monthly early, quarterly as rhythm stabilizes) should produce judgment, not theater: honest numbers, real dilemmas, explicit asks — and leave with decisions and owners. The deck goes out days early so meeting time spends on discussion; any meeting spent reading slides aloud is a failure of preparation.

```text
good board pack:  metrics vs plan (same definitions monthly) · wins + misses, honestly
                  2–3 strategic questions with options · explicit asks · financials appendix
```

Frame each dilemma as options with trade-offs and a recommendation — boards decide best when asked to judge, not to brainstorm from zero. And mine the room: directors have seen your problem at five other companies; the question you are embarrassed to ask is usually the valuable one.

The classic mistakes:

- Performance theater. Vanity metrics, buried bad news, surprise-free narratives — discovered eventually, trust destroyed permanently. Bad news early with a plan beats ambushes every time.
- No asks. Presenting without requesting (intros, hiring help, decision approval) wastes the highest-value function of the room. Every meeting ends with owned asks and dates.
- Deck the night before. Directors skim on arrival; discussion stays shallow. Materials days early, pre-reads confirmed — or reschedule rather than waste the quorum.
- Everything confidential shared freely. Board packs contain the company's secrets; leaks (often accidental, via observers and forwards) happen. Mark sensitivity, limit distribution, track who holds what.
- Skipping meetings when things are bad. Exactly when oversight and help matter most. Bad quarters need *more* board engagement, structured around options — never avoidance.

**The standard:** honest pack early, dilemmas framed, asks explicit, minutes recorded. A board run this way becomes the company's sharpest strategic tool instead of its most expensive audience.
