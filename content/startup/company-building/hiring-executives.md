Hiring executives means bringing senior leaders — a CFO, VP Engineering, CRO, COO — above the early team, usually after PMF and revenue scale make founder-doing-everything the binding constraint. It is where companies both buy speed and manufacture damage at industrial scale; the failure rate is high because the job is not "senior individual contributor, more titles," and the candidates most persuasive at interviewing are often least suited to your stage.

The classic failure patterns:

- **Too early.** Hiring a big-company executive to build a function that does not yet exist; they arrive with a playbook for a hundred-person org and a twenty-person reality, and the culture reads it as corporate tourism.
- **Halo hiring.** A famous brand's VP is proof they survived inside a machine, not that they can build one. Reference checks that ask only "were they smart?" confirm the résumé; the useful questions are operational — what did they personally build, who followed them out, what broke after they left.
- **Title without scope.** An executive without real decision rights becomes the founder's translator; both sides grow resentful fast.
- **Compensation shock.** Market-rate packages for an executive can dwarf early-employee pay ([startup compensation](/t/startup-compensation) tension), and equity splits decided in a meeting without a range framework poison the room ([equity split](/t/equity-split) gravity cuts both ways).

What makes one work: a written 90-day shape (what exists by end of first quarter — function, hires, metrics owned), founder agreement on exactly what the executive decides alone, one direct internal sponsor (the CEO), and a compensation structure with the same discipline as everyone else's ([vesting](/t/vesting-schedule) with performance milestones for risky hires, not ceremonial grants).

When not to do it: pre-PMF, where strategy changes weekly make any specialist premature; when the actual constraint is founder bandwidth rather than capability (fractional advisors, or growing an internal leader, can be cheaper and stickier); and when the motivation is prestige or investor optics rather than the missing function. Executive hiring is a bet that the bottleneck is leadership; check the bottleneck first.
