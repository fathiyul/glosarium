The **board of directors** oversees the company on shareholders' behalf: hiring and firing the CEO, approving financings and exits, and governing through formal votes. It typically forms at the first priced round — founder(s), investor director(s), and often an independent — and its quality shapes the company's hardest decisions for years.

```text
typical early board:  founder-CEO + lead investor + mutually-agreed independent
powers that matter:   hire/fire CEO · approve financings, sales, budgets · protect preferred rights
```

A good board is a strategic asset: pattern recognition across dozens of companies, hiring networks, fundraising credibility, and a forcing function for honest thinking. A bad board is governance theater that slows decisions and leaks information — choose members like co-founders, because unwinding them is harder.

The classic mistakes:

- Filling seats with money, not value. Any check writer offered a board seat "to close the round" becomes a permanent governance voice without governance value. Reserve seats for contributors.
- Founder-majority complacency. Control without oversight breeds blind spots no one names. An independent director who challenges you is protection, not threat.
- Board as reporting audience. Performing good news monthly wastes the room's real value (judgment on hard calls). Bring dilemmas, not just dashboards — see [board meetings](/t/board-meetings).
- Ignoring fiduciary reality. Directors owe duties to *all* shareholders, and common-vs-preferred conflicts get real in down rounds and sales. Understand duties before they bind, with counsel.
- Observer sprawl. Every small investor demanding observer seats creates a crowded, leaky room. Limit observers strictly; information rights scale better than attendance.

**Compose deliberately:** people whose judgment you want in crises, whose networks open doors, and who will tell you hard truths early. The board you build in good times governs you in bad ones.
