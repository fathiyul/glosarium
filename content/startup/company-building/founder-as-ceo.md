The **founder-CEO's** job compresses to four duties nobody else can do: set direction (vision translated into strategy and focus), attract exceptional people, ensure money never runs out (fundraising, burn discipline), and make the decisions no one else can (pivots, layoffs, kills). Everything else — however enjoyable — is delegation waiting to happen.

```text
CEO calendar should show:  customers + candidates + capital + critical decisions
CEO calendar should NOT show: routine ops, deep code reviews, expense approvals
```

Technical founders systematically underinvest the job's extrovert half: selling (customers, candidates, investors), repeating the story until sick of it, and managing energy and mood as company infrastructure — the team's emotional weather comes from you more than any process.

The classic mistakes:

- Coding instead of CEO-ing. The comfortable work (architecture, features) devouring the necessary work (hiring, selling, fundraising). Schedule CEO work first; code with leftovers, not vice versa.
- Absent vision repetition. Stating direction once and assuming retention. Teams need the why retold quarterly as people join and markets shift — repetition is the job, not a failure of listening.
- Money surprises. Discovering runway crises instead of managing them. Cash, burn and fundraise timing are CEO-owned numbers reviewed weekly, delegated never.
- Avoiding the hard personnel calls. Underperforming executives, toxic stars, co-founder drift — delayed quarters past obvious. Speed on people decisions is kindness to everyone including the departed ([letting go](/t/letting-people-go)).
- No outside input. CEO is the loneliest information-poor role: everyone filters upward. Coaches, peer groups and brutally honest directors are infrastructure, not luxury.

**Measure the week:** direction set? Great people hired/unblocked? Money secured? Hard calls made? Four yeses is a CEO week; brilliant code shipped is a hobby with opportunity cost.
