**Startup compensation** balances cash you barely have against equity that might be worthless — for people who could earn more elsewhere. The package has four levers: salary (discounted but livable), equity (the upside story), benefits (health, flexibility, leave), and mission/growth (the non-financial pay). Candidates weigh the bundle, not the salary line.

```text
offer:  70–85% market salary + meaningful options + real benefits + growth story
sell:   the total trajectory (cash now + wealth if we win + career acceleration either way)
```

Publish bands and philosophy early — even roughly. Ad-hoc offers breed inequity, resentment and negotiation theater that small teams cannot afford. Transparency scales; secrecy compounds.

The classic mistakes:

- Competing on cash with giants. Matching big-tech salaries burns runway for hires who joined for money and leave for more of it. Compete on mission, growth and upside — concede cash deliberately, not accidentally.
- Equity handed out opaquely. Widely varying grants for similar roles, unexplained, discovered over lunch. Bands per level, documented rationale, communicated openly.
- Ignoring benefits until forced. Health coverage, leave, parental support — delayed until a key hire demands them, then scrambled. Basic competitive benefits from the first hires; they cost less than one lost candidate.
- No refresh or growth story. Year-four employees fully vested with flat salaries walk. Compensation needs a multi-year arc: refreshes, level-ups, growing cash as revenue allows.
- One-size-fits-all. Engineers, salespeople and operators value different mixes (cash vs upside vs stability). Tailor within bands; rigid equality ignores real differences in risk and market.

**Review yearly:** bands against market, equity refresh health, benefits competitiveness. Compensation philosophy is culture written in money — write it deliberately.
