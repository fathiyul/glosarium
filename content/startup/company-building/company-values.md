**Company values** are written principles that guide decisions at scale — what "good" looks like when founders are not in the room to judge. Done right they compress culture into portable rules: hiring filters, tiebreakers in debates, permission slips for hard calls. Done wrong they are wall art everyone ignores.

```text
real value:   "Disagree openly, commit fully" → cited in actual debates, used in reviews
wall art:     "Integrity. Excellence. Synergy." → believed by no one, referenced never
```

Good values are specific, opinionated and slightly costly — they rule *out* as much as in. "Move fast" that tolerates careful deliberation means nothing; "default to action, ask forgiveness for reversible calls" decides things. Write from lived behavior (what the best people already do), not aspiration.

The classic mistakes:

- Generic virtue lists. Honesty, respect, excellence — true of every company and decisive in none. Values must discriminate: a candidate who disagrees should self-select out.
- Launch-and-forget. Values announced once at an all-hands, never referenced in hiring, reviews or firings. Embed in loops: interview scorecards, promotion packets, post-mortems, terminations.
- Too many. Twelve values equal zero values — nobody holds twelve in mind during a hard call. Three to five, memorable, ordered by priority for conflicts between them.
- Founders exempt. Values the leadership visibly violates teach that values are lies — worse than none at all. Founders go first, publicly, especially when costly.
- Copied values. Another company's principles transplanted wholesale fit like borrowed clothes. Derive from your team's actual best moments, in your own words.

**Test quarterly:** can everyone name them? Were they cited in a real decision this month? Was someone rewarded or exited over them? Values alive in decisions compound into culture; others decorate.
