A sustainable pace is a rhythm of work you can maintain for years, not just for a sprint. It means working hard in focused stretches and taking real breaks, rather than running at full speed all the time. Steady effort is usually easier to keep up than bursts of extra hours, so it's worth planning for.

Some practical signs of a pace that's too fast: evenings and weekends are regularly spent on work, you rarely finish the day feeling done, and you feel anxious about messages when you're off. Some signs of a sustainable one: you can take a day off without worrying about what happens, and your work quality stays steady.

```
Unsustainable: 60-hour weeks for two months, then a week of recovery.
Sustainable:   a steady week with clear end times and one real day off.
```

Pace is something to discuss, not just decide alone. Raise it in a [1:1](/t/career-conversations) if deadlines or workload don't match what the team can do.

The classic mistake is treating a crunch as normal. Short bursts can be necessary, but if they become routine, people can burn out, and the quality of the work can slip. The early signs are covered in [burnout](/t/burnout).
