A behavioral interview asks about things you've done in the past: "tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate," or "describe a bug you couldn't track down." The STAR format gives your answer a clear structure: Situation, Task, Action and Result.

```
Situation: Our checkout failed for some customers after a release.
Task:      I was asked to find the cause before the next deploy.
Action:    I reproduced it with a test account, found a cached old config, and wrote a fix with a test.
Result:    Failures stopped, and I added a check so the config is validated at startup.
```

Keep the answer specific and about you. Interviewers want to know what you did, not what the team did. Describe the action in enough detail to be credible, and the result in a way you can name, even if it's small.

The classic mistake is giving a vague story with no result, or spending most of the time on background. Aim for about a minute or two per answer, and prepare a few stories ahead of time that cover different themes, such as conflict, failure, and learning something new. Your [brag document](/t/brag-document) is a good source. For the other rounds, see [technical interviews](/t/technical-interview).
