title: "Senior Engineer"
summary: "Owning systems and raising the team around you."
levels: { backend: senior, frontend: senior, data-engineer: senior , data-scientist: senior }

A senior engineer is often expected to own outcomes beyond an individual implementation: clarify ambiguous work, make trade-offs visible, help others succeed, and keep systems maintainable and operable. The exact scope varies by organization, so use local expectations rather than title stereotypes.

For example, owning a service change includes considering its callers, rollout, monitoring, and failure behavior—not only merging code. A senior engineer may notice that two teams are solving the same problem and bring them together before duplicated solutions become expensive. This influence comes from sound judgment and collaboration, not from having the final answer in every discussion.

Senior work still includes technical contribution, but the leverage may come from reviews, design, mentoring, or reducing a recurring risk. Avoid becoming the only person who can change a system; share context and build other owners. Delegating or teaching is not a sign that your technical skills are no longer useful.

Backend seniors can show ownership through rollout plans and clear service boundaries. Frontend seniors demonstrate it through accessible, testable interfaces and honest handling of edge cases. Data seniors earn trust through validated models and careful backfill or migration practice. In each case, write down decisions so others can follow the reasoning. Ask teammates whether your reviews and designs make their work easier, and adjust where they do not. Keep asking whose work became easier because of your help.

Ask for feedback on the outcomes and behaviors your organization expects. See [mid-level engineer](/t/mid-level-engineer), [tech lead](/t/tech-lead), and [staff engineer](/t/staff-engineer).
