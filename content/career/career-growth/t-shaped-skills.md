title: "T-Shaped Skills"
summary: "Broad knowledge with deep expertise in one area."

T-shaped skills describe a profile with breadth across several areas and deeper expertise in one or a few. The “shape” is a metaphor, not a hiring rubric or a requirement that every engineer fit the same pattern.

Breadth helps you collaborate across boundaries. A frontend engineer who understands API constraints can make better interface trade-offs; a data engineer who understands product events can catch instrumentation gaps. Depth lets you solve harder problems in an area and recognize when an apparent shortcut creates risk.

You can build breadth through adjacent work, reviews, pairing, and reading system documentation. Build depth by owning a problem over time, studying fundamentals, and learning from operational outcomes. Trying to learn everything can dilute focus, while specializing too narrowly may make it harder to adapt or explain your work to partners.

If you focus on backend work, depth might mean storage or distributed behavior while breadth covers API and deployment basics. Frontend depth could be interaction or performance, with enough backend literacy to negotiate contracts. Data depth might be modeling or pipeline reliability, with enough product context to choose the right grain. Pick one area to deepen through sustained ownership, and use reviews and pairing to keep the broad edges honest.

Choose skills based on the problems your team and users need solved, and revisit the balance as your role changes. You do not need to advertise yourself as a generalist or specialist to make this useful.
