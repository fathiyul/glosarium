summary: "Two parallel career paths, and how to choose between them."

The individual-contributor (IC) and management tracks are different ways to contribute, not a ranking where one is the next step after the other. ICs focus on technical work and its broader effects; managers focus on enabling people and teams through coaching, hiring, planning, and organizational decisions. Both still require communication and judgment.

Before choosing, learn what the roles actually involve at your organization. A senior IC may spend less time coding directly and more time on architecture, risk, and alignment. A manager may still understand technical work but is accountable for team conditions and people processes. Trying management because it seems like the only route to influence can lead to a poor fit.

You can test interests through mentoring, project leadership, or temporary responsibilities without treating them as a permanent commitment. Ask practitioners what their week looks like and what work they have stopped doing. Role definitions differ between companies, so compare responsibilities rather than titles.

Backend ICs might shape service architecture while managers handle staffing and delivery risk. Frontend ICs may steward interaction quality while managers coordinate design and release commitments. Data ICs often own modeling direction while managers align producers and consumers. Talk to people doing each role about what their week actually contains. Choose based on the work you want to sustain, not on prestige or the assumption that management is the only way to grow.

Neither path is free of trade-offs: management can involve difficult personnel decisions, while IC influence may require sustained cross-team alignment without direct authority.
