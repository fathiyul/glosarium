A promotion is a formal change in level or role, usually based on sustained evidence that your work meets the expectations of the next level. Processes vary: some organizations use a committee and written packet, while others rely more on manager review. Learn the local criteria instead of guessing from title alone.

Ask what “next-level scope” means in your organization and request examples of evidence. This might include owning a cross-team outcome, reducing a recurring operational risk, or helping others deliver—not simply taking on more tickets. Keep concise notes on outcomes, decisions, and feedback so a review can draw on more than recent memory.

Promotion is not fully under your control. Timing, available roles, calibration, and business needs may affect the decision. Clarify what is missing and what opportunity would let you demonstrate it; do not accept indefinite extra work without an honest discussion of scope and recognition. A fair process should make expectations understandable and allow questions.

Your manager should help create opportunities and give timely feedback, but you can help by communicating your goals and impact. Compare expectations against your organization's [career ladder](/t/career-ladder), and see [career conversations](/t/career-conversations) and [senior engineer](/t/senior-engineer).

Backend engineers can document how an API or reliability improvement changed life for callers or operators. Frontend engineers can show how an accessibility or performance change affected real use. Data engineers can trace how a modeling or quality fix improved trust in downstream decisions. Promotion packets are easier to evaluate when each example names the problem, your contribution, and the outcome. Keep the tone factual, and ask a peer to check that the narrative matches the work.
