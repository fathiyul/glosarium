A junior engineer is early in their career and is expected to deliver well-defined tasks with guidance. The work usually comes with a clear description, and the goal is to finish it well, learn the codebase, and get better at working with the team.

The most useful habits at this level are asking good questions and finishing what you start. When you're stuck, try for a while, write down what you've tried, and then ask. Asking early for a short time is often better than struggling for a long time without progress.

```
Stuck on: why the export test fails only on CI.
Tried:    reproduced locally, checked the timezone setting, compared CI and local versions.
Asking:   Do you know if CI uses a different timezone? Here's the failing run.
```

Don't wait to be told what to learn. Read code in the areas you work on, review pull requests, and ask for feedback. A [mentor](/t/finding-a-mentor) can speed this up a lot.

The classic mistake is staying quiet to avoid looking inexperienced, or the reverse, asking questions before trying anything. Both slow the team down. Ask clear questions, show what you've already done, and treat each task as a chance to learn how the team works.
