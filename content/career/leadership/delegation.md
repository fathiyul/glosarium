---
title: "Delegation"
summary: "Handing off work in a way that grows others."
levels: { backend: senior, frontend: senior, data-engineer: senior , tech-entrepreneur: growth , data-scientist: senior }
---

Delegation transfers responsibility for a piece of work while making the goal, boundaries, and support clear. It is not simply assigning a task and disappearing, nor does it mean the manager or lead gives up accountability for the outcome.

A good handoff explains why the work matters, what result is expected, which constraints are fixed, and where the person can make decisions. For example, ask an engineer to propose a rollout plan and own the implementation, while agreeing that data-loss risk requires review before cutover. Schedule check-ins based on risk and experience, not as constant status surveillance.

Delegation can take more time initially than doing a familiar task yourself. That investment can build capability and reduce single-person dependencies, but delegating without authority or context sets someone up to fail. Match responsibility to access and decision rights, and be available when new information changes the task.

Backend delegation might hand over a service migration with explicit compatibility and rollback boundaries. Frontend delegation could give ownership of a flow redesign while keeping accessibility requirements fixed. Data delegation may transfer a pipeline with clear validation and consumer expectations. Check understanding by asking the person to restate the goal and risks. Stay available for new information, and review the result against the original intent rather than personal style.

Afterward, give credit to the person who did the work and discuss what they learned. Backend, frontend, and data leads should avoid delegating only routine or invisible tasks to the same people.
