---
title: "Sponsorship"
summary: "Using your influence to create opportunities for others."
levels: { backend: staff, frontend: staff, data-engineer: staff }
---

Sponsorship is using your position or influence to create opportunities for someone else and to make their contributions visible to decision-makers. It differs from advice alone: a sponsor may recommend someone for a project, introduce them to stakeholders, or advocate for their work when they are not in the room.

For example, if an engineer has built expertise in a reliability problem, a sponsor might put them forward to lead a cross-team review and ensure they receive credit for the outcome. Effective sponsorship should reflect the person's goals and readiness, not use them as a proxy for the sponsor's own agenda.

Access to visible work and advocacy can be uneven. Managers and senior engineers should notice who gets stretch opportunities and who is doing essential but unseen work. Sponsorship does not guarantee promotion, and it should not replace fair evaluation or transparent criteria.

Backend sponsors might recommend someone to lead a reliability review. Frontend sponsors can create room for a colleague to own a visible interaction. Data sponsors may connect a teammate with a cross-team modeling effort. Match the opportunity to the person's goals and current capacity. Stay involved enough to unblock, then step back and let them own the result. Check back on progress without taking over the work.

Backend, frontend, and data engineers can sponsor across specialties by making relevant expertise visible and opening access to meaningful work. Ask what support the person wants, respect their boundaries, and follow through. See [glue work](/t/glue-work), [promotion](/t/promotion), and [delegation](/t/delegation).
