summary: "A picture of where the technology should be in a few years."

A technical vision describes a future state the organization wants its technology to support and the principles that guide movement toward it. It connects user or business needs to system direction without prescribing every future implementation detail.

A useful vision might state that teams should be able to deploy independently while preserving reliable data contracts. That gives teams a direction for architecture and platform investment, while leaving room to choose tools and sequence work as evidence changes. A list of technologies without the problem they solve is not a vision.

Visions can become detached from reality if they assume unlimited time or ignore existing customers and systems. Show the gap between today and the desired state, identify the most important constraints, and define signals that would cause the direction to change. Explain what should remain stable as well as what may evolve.

Backend vision might describe independent deployability with dependable contracts. Frontend vision could aim for coherent, accessible experiences across surfaces. Data vision may emphasize trustworthy, well-described datasets. Show how near-term investments move toward that state. Repeat the message through funding and review choices, and revise it when customer needs or constraints change materially. Tie the next funding choice back to the direction.

Backend, frontend, and data engineers can contribute constraints and opportunities from their domains; leaders need to synthesize them into a coherent direction. Communicate the vision repeatedly through concrete decisions, not just a presentation.
