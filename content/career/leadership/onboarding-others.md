---
title: "Onboarding New Engineers"
summary: "Helping new teammates become productive."
levels: { backend: senior, frontend: senior, data-engineer: senior , data-analyst: senior }
---

Onboarding new engineers is helping someone learn the product, systems, people, and working practices they need to contribute safely. It starts before their first task and continues as they encounter unfamiliar parts of the organization.

Provide a clear first-week path: working environment setup, access requests, a system map, a person to ask, and a small starter task with a real outcome. A good starter task exercises the normal development and review path without putting a new teammate on a high-risk change alone. Keep documentation current when onboarding reveals that instructions are missing or stale.

Do not assume that a new engineer will ask every question. They may not know what they do not know or who owns a system. Pair them with people who can explain context, but avoid making one teammate the permanent help desk. Access should follow least privilege and increase as responsibilities require it.

Backend onboarding benefits from a map of services, owners, and safe change paths. Frontend onboarding needs pointers to design sources, test setups, and review norms. Data onboarding should explain sources, contracts, and how to validate a change before backfill. Give newcomers a real first task and a person to ask. Afterward, fold their questions back into the docs so the next person meets fewer surprises.

Backend, frontend, and data teams should explain how their services connect and what safe operational practices look like. Ask the new person what remains confusing and improve the process rather than treating confusion as an individual failure.
