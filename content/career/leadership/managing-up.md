---
title: "Managing Up"
summary: "Keeping your manager informed and aligned."
levels: { backend: senior, frontend: senior, data-engineer: senior , data-scientist: senior }
---

Managing up is keeping your manager informed and making it easier for both of you to work toward shared outcomes. It does not mean manipulating your manager or taking responsibility for their job; it means communicating needs, risks, and progress in a way they can act on.

A useful update may cover what changed, what is blocked, what decision you need, and by when. For example: “The integration is ready except for an access decision; if we do not hear back by Thursday, the pilot moves. Can you help identify the decision owner?” This gives a clear request instead of expecting your manager to infer it from a status color.

Managers vary in the detail and cadence they need. Ask what format is useful and agree how urgent issues should be raised. Do not hide bad news until a deadline; early visibility gives the team more options. If priorities conflict, describe the trade-off and ask which outcome should move.

Backend engineers can translate a scaling risk into delivery options and dates. Frontend engineers might frame an accessibility gap as user impact and remediation choices. Data engineers can explain a source delay in terms of decisions it blocks. Match the update to what your manager can decide or unblock. If a request stalls, propose a default action and a deadline for objection so work does not wait silently.

Backend, frontend, and data engineers can make invisible technical risk legible without overwhelming their manager with implementation details. Keep a written record of decisions that affect scope or ownership.
