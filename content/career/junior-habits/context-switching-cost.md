Context switching is moving from one task to another. Each switch costs something: you have to rebuild the picture of the problem in your head, remember where you stopped, and find what you were about to do next. That cost is easy to miss, because each switch feels small.

A typical day shows the effect. You're halfway through a bug, a message arrives, you answer it, then a quick review request comes in, and when you return to the bug you have to rebuild the whole chain of reasoning. Several interruptions in a row can leave you with a lot of activity and little finished work.

```
Morning: 4 projects touched, 0 finished
Afternoon: 1 project, one clear step done
```

Some switching is unavoidable, especially on a team where people ask for help. The habit to build is batching: group similar small tasks together, answer messages at set times, and close the open loop before you move on when you can.

The classic mistake is treating being responsive as the same as being productive. Quick replies matter, but so does having time to think about hard problems. Protect a block of time for focused work, as described in [deep work](/t/deep-work).
