The first weeks in a new job aren't about proving how much you know. They're about **getting set up, learning how things work and getting a first small win
shipped**.

## Week one: get running and meet people

- **Set up your environment** and get the project running locally ([dev environment setup](/t/dev-environment-setup)). Note every missing or wrong step, then fix the docs.
  It's a real contribution, and your fresh eyes are the best test of them.
- **Get access** to everything you need (repos, chat, tickets, cloud consoles, CI), early, since access requests can take days.
- **Meet the team** and your manager. Ask what's expected in the first 30, 60 and 90 days, and how you'll know you're doing well.
- **Find your go-to people**: a buddy or mentor for everyday questions ([finding a mentor](/t/finding-a-mentor)).
- **Learn the rhythm**: standups, planning, release days, on-call, where decisions get made.

## Early weeks: learn and ship something small

- **Ship a small change in the first week or two**, such as a docs fix, a small bug or a test. It teaches the whole path from ticket to production: branch, review, CI,
  deploy. It also builds confidence.
- **Read code and docs** with a question in mind ([reading a codebase](/t/reading-a-codebase)).
- **Ask questions**, and write the answers down ([asking good questions](/t/asking-good-questions), [engineering journal](/t/engineering-journal)).
- **Follow the team's conventions** before suggesting changes ([following conventions](/t/following-conventions)).
- **Observe before you propose.** Keep a list of things that seem odd, and revisit them in a few weeks when you understand the context. Some will prove sensible.

## Habits that help

- **Take notes** on people, terms and systems. You'll forget a lot of what you hear.
- **Don't struggle silently.** Say when you're blocked ([when to ask for help](/t/when-to-ask-for-help)).
- **Be honest about what you don't know.** Pretending slows your learning.
- **Check in with your manager regularly**, and ask for feedback early, not just at the review.
- **Pace yourself.** It's a lot of new information. Tiredness is normal.

Feeling slow or lost at first is expected. Almost everyone does ([impostor syndrome](/t/impostor-syndrome)). In a few weeks, the map fills in.
