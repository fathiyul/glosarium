Backlog refinement is the ongoing work of making upcoming items ready to start. The team clarifies what an item means, splits large items into smaller ones, adds acceptance criteria, and estimates effort. Some teams call it grooming.

A refined item usually answers a few questions: what problem it solves, how we'll know it's done, and what it depends on. A vague item like "improve search" becomes something the team can test only after someone asks what improve means, such as "search results load within the response-time target we agreed on, for the catalog sizes we actually have."

Refinement is usually a short, regular activity, not one big meeting. Work on the top of the backlog a little ahead of when it's needed for [sprint](/t/sprint) planning, and keep it focused on the next few items.

The classic mistake is turning refinement into a design session where the team solves the whole feature. That drags on and delays the work. Leave the detailed design to the person doing the work, and use refinement to confirm the item is clear enough to start. Splitting large work into [epics](/t/epic) and stories is one of the most useful outputs.
