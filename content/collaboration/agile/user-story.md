A user story describes a small piece of functionality from the point of view of the person who uses it. The usual template has three parts: who the user is, what they want to do, and why it matters to them.

```
As a returning customer,
I want to save my card for next time,
so that I don't have to type it at every checkout.
```

The "so that" part is the one people skip, and it's the one that helps most. It explains the value, which lets the team pick a better solution than the one the story first suggests.

A story is a conversation starter, not a complete specification. Add acceptance criteria, the conditions that show the story is done, during [backlog refinement](/t/backlog-refinement). Large stories that won't fit in one [sprint](/t/sprint) belong under an [epic](/t/epic).

The classic mistake is writing the template without thinking about the user. "As a system, I want to store a card token" describes a task, not a user need. If you can't name the user or the reason, ask before you start the work.
