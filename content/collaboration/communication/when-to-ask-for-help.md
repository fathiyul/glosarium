Juniors often get this wrong in one of two directions: asking **too soon** (before thinking at all), or **too late**, silently stuck for days because they don't want to
look incompetent. Both cost the team.

## The middle path: time-box it

Decide how long you'll try on your own, then ask. A rough guide for a typical problem is 30 to 60 minutes of real effort. For something blocking everyone,
sooner. Adjust by the situation:

- **Block someone's work, or production is affected?** Ask now.
- **Learning problem** where the struggle is the point? Give it longer ([productive struggle](/t/productive-struggle)).
- **Deadline close?** Shorten the time-box.
- **A senior would solve it in two minutes** (an environment quirk, a team convention)? Ask. Struggling here teaches nothing.

## Before you ask

- Reread the error, and search ([searching effectively](/t/searching-effectively)).
- Check the docs, the README and the code.
- Explain the problem out loud ([rubber duck](/t/rubber-duck-debugging)). It often solves it.
- Write down what you tried, ready for your question ([asking good questions](/t/asking-good-questions)).

## Signs it's time

- You're repeating the same things, or going in circles.
- You've started making random changes hoping something works.
- You've spent your time-box and can't say what to try next.
- The task is clearly bigger or different than described.
- You're avoiding the task because of the blocker.

## Making it easy

- **Pick the right person and channel.** The team channel for general questions. The person who owns the area for specific ones.
- **Ask for direction, not for the whole solution**: "Where should I look?" or "What am I missing?" You learn more and need less of their time.
- **Respect focus**: if they're mid-task, send the written question and carry on with something else
  ([async communication](/t/async-communication)).
- **Say what you've learned**, and apply the answer.

A healthy team **expects** questions, especially from new people ([psychological safety](/t/psychological-safety)). Nobody expects you to know everything. They expect you to
make progress, and to tell them when you can't.
