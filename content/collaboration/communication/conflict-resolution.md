---
title: "Resolving Conflict"
summary: "Working through disagreements productively."
levels: { backend: senior, frontend: senior, data-engineer: senior , data-scientist: senior }
---

Conflict resolution is working through a disagreement so people can understand the issue, choose a path, and continue collaborating. Conflict may concern goals, constraints, responsibilities, or communication—not just personality.

Separate observations from interpretations. “The API changed after the frontend integrated” is more actionable than “the backend team is unreliable.” Ask each person what outcome they need, what evidence they have, and which constraints cannot be ignored. A short direct conversation can clear up an assumption that has grown through chat messages.

Not every disagreement needs consensus. If a decision has an owner, gather relevant input, make the trade-off explicit, and record the decision and unresolved risk. Escalate when the issue involves harassment, safety, policy, or a power imbalance that makes direct resolution inappropriate. Do not use “conflict” language to dismiss a valid technical concern.

Backend, frontend, and data engineers should translate differences into interfaces, ownership, and user impact where possible. Keep the conversation focused on the problem and agreements, not on winning. Follow up to confirm the chosen action happened. See [psychological safety](/t/psychological-safety), [disagree and commit](/t/disagree-and-commit), and [stakeholder management](/t/stakeholder-management).

Choose the channel and amount of detail for the people who need to act, then make important outcomes findable later. If a conversation changes scope or ownership, record that change rather than relying on memory. Backend developers can explain service impact, frontend developers can clarify interaction concerns, and data engineers can make lineage or measurement implications visible.

After trying it, ask whether people had enough context to act and revise the agreement if it created extra coordination or left someone out.
